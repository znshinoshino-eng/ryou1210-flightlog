Arriving Late at Narita or Haneda: How to Get into Tokyo

Airport guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: if your flight lands late, check the last train and bus before you fly, using each airport's official route search. If you'll miss them, your choices are a taxi (Narita has fixed fares to Tokyo, but they're expensive, and there's a late-night surcharge from 22:00 to 5:00), a pre-booked private transfer, or staying overnight at the airport and taking the first train.

Late arrivals are one of the most stressful parts of a first trip. Here's what the airports themselves say, so you can decide before you land.

Step 1: check the last train before you fly

Timetables change, so don't rely on a time you saw in a blog post. Both airports have official tools:

  • Haneda: the airport website has a route search for each terminal (T1, T2 and T3). It shows departure times and fares to major stations and hotels.
  • Narita: the airport website has separate pages for trains, buses and taxis, and a page on using the terminals at night and early in the morning.

Remember that immigration, baggage claim and customs can take a while after a long-haul flight. If your landing time is close to the last train, plan as if you'll miss it. Our guides to Narita to Tokyo and Haneda to Tokyo explain the normal train and bus options.

Taxis from Narita: fixed fares

Narita Airport lists fixed taxi fares to Tokyo's 23 wards and nearby areas. These are the fares before expressway tolls, which you pay on top:

  • A late-night and early-morning surcharge applies from 22:00 to 5:00.
  • The fixed fare assumes an expressway route. If you ask for a route without expressways, the meter is used instead.
  • No reservation is needed. Confirm the fare with the driver before you get in.

Shown at the time of writing (October 2026). Check the airport's taxi page for the latest fares.

Taxis from Haneda: flat-rate taxis

Haneda Airport says you can use flat-rate taxis between the airport and most of Tokyo and Kanagawa, with some exceptions. Toll fees are added on top, and some areas use the normal meter. The airport doesn't list the amounts itself and points you to the taxi companies and the Tokyo and Kanagawa taxi associations. Haneda has two kinds of taxi stands: regular stands, and separate stands for taxis booked through an app or by phone.

A pre-booked transfer

If you're arriving late with a family or lots of luggage, a private transfer booked in advance means a driver is waiting for you, whatever time you clear customs. You can compare options and prices for your date with Narita transfers on Klook (affiliate link) or Haneda transfers on Klook (affiliate link). Prices depend on the car size and destination. For more on how these work, see our guide to private car transfers in Tokyo.

Staying overnight at Narita

If you land very late, waiting for the first train can be the cheapest choice. Narita Airport says:

  • Shops and facilities are available 24 hours a day in each terminal, but entrance hours differ by floor. In Terminal 1, the 1F arrivals area and B1F are open 24 hours. In Terminal 2, the 2F domestic arrivals area, 1F and B1F are open 24 hours. In Terminal 3, the 2F and 1F are open 24 hours.
  • 24-hour convenience stores: LAWSON in Terminal 1 (B1F) and Terminal 3, and 7-Eleven in Terminal 2 (B1F).
  • 24-hour restaurants: Yoshinoya in the Terminal 2 Main Building and MATSUYA in Terminal 3.
  • A capsule hotel in Parking P-2, directly connected to Terminal 2, is open 24 hours. The airport recommends booking ahead, as it can be full.

Before a night at the airport, it helps to have a working phone connection. See our eSIM and power bank guide.

Quick checklist

  • Check the last train or bus from your terminal before your trip.
  • If it's close, decide on a backup now: taxi, pre-booked transfer, or a night at the airport.
  • Carry some yen in cash as a backup.
  • Tell your hotel if you'll arrive after midnight.
