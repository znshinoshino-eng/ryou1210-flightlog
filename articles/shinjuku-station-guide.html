Shinjuku Station Guide: Exits, Lines and Busta Shinjuku

Station guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: "Shinjuku Station" is really several stations run by different companies, joined by passages. Work out three things before you go: which company runs your train, which side (east, west or south) you want to come out on, and which gate gets you there. For the Busta Shinjuku bus terminal, use JR's New South Gate: the terminal is directly connected to it.

I work on Japan's railways, and Shinjuku is the station where I see the most visitors standing still and staring at signs. It's big, but it's not random. Once you know how it's put together, it gets much easier.

Who runs what at Shinjuku

Your ticket or IC card works across all of these, but each company has its own gates. If you are changing between companies, look for the transfer gates rather than walking out to the street. Our guide to how Tokyo's train companies fit together explains why, and IC cards make these changes much simpler.

One more name to watch: Seibu-Shinjuku is a different station. JR East says it's about 400 m away, a 7–8 minute walk, and the easiest JR gate for it is the East Gate on the basement level (B1).

JR's gates, and which one to use

JR Shinjuku has 10 gates: South, West, Central East, Central West, East, Southeast, New South, Koshu-kaido, Miraina Tower, and the Odakyu transfer gate. Most are open from the first train to the last. JR East notes that the Miraina Tower Gate and the Odakyu transfer gate have no staff; you use an intercom there if you need help.

If you're using an older map, the names may look slightly different. In July 2020 JR East renamed six gates, for example "East Exit Gate" became "East Gate" and "Central West Exit Gate" became "Central West Gate". At the same time it opened the East-West Free Passage, a wide public walkway (about 25 m wide and 100 m long) that links the east and west sides, so you can cross the station without a ticket.

Coming from the Keio, Odakyu or Toei Shinjuku lines and want the east side? JR East's own suggestion is to walk through the East-West Free Passage.

Busta Shinjuku: the bus terminal above the station

Busta Shinjuku (officially the Shinjuku Expressway Bus Terminal) is Shinjuku's highway bus terminal, and you can also buy Airport Limousine bus tickets there. The terminal says all of its bus stops are gathered at the South Exit of the station.

  • 4F: departures. This floor has the waiting room, the information counter, ticket counters and machines, coin lockers, an ATM and a convenience store.
  • 3F: arrivals.
  • 2F: pedestrian area and station connection.

Getting there from JR: the terminal says it's about 2 minutes from the New South Gate. That gate is open from 5:45 to 24:00. Outside those hours, use the South Gate, which is about a 4-minute walk.

The terminal itself says these times are only a guide, so leave yourself extra time, especially with luggage.

Useful hours at the time of writing (October 2026):

  • Waiting room and convenience store: 3:30 to 25:00 (that's 1:00 the next morning)
  • Information counter: 7:00 to 23:00
  • Staffed ticket counter: hours depend on the booking system, for example 5:40 to 23:45 for Highwaybus.com and 4:20 to 22:50 for Airport Limousine
  • Coin lockers (4F): from 30 minutes before the first bus to the last departure

Left something behind at the terminal? Lost property found there is held by the terminal (phone 03-6380-4794, 9:00 to 17:00). For items left on a train, see what to do if you lose something on a train.

Tickets, help desks and lockers

  • JR ticket offices (Midori no Madoguchi): there are two, one on B1 in the East-West passage near the East and West gates, and one at the South Gate on 2F. Both are open 6:00 to 22:00.
  • JR EAST Travel Service Center: on your right after you leave the New South Gate. Open 8:00 to 19:00, every day. You can buy JR East passes, pick up tickets booked on JR-EAST Train Reservation, and exchange currency. If you're still deciding on a pass, read whether a rail pass is worth it.
  • Coin lockers: JR has lockers both inside and outside the gates, including near the South Gate and the East Gate. Many are Multi eCube lockers, which you can check for free space and reserve online (up to one month ahead) on the Multi eCube website.

Shinjuku's lockers are busy, and dragging a big suitcase through the crowds is no fun. Luggage forwarding to your next hotel is often the easier choice.

Going to Hakone from Shinjuku

Shinjuku is the Tokyo starting point for Odakyu's Romancecar limited express. Odakyu says it takes about 80 minutes to Hakone-Yumoto, and all seats are reserved. You can buy tickets online through EMot Online Tickets, or at Odakyu station windows and ticket machines. Our Mt Fuji and Hakone day trips guide covers the trip in more detail.

If you'd like to book a Hakone transport pass before you go, the Hakone Freepass on Klook (affiliate link) was rated 4.6 from about 11,700 reviews, from US$44.95, on Klook at the time of writing. Prices vary by date and option.

Tips from the railway side

  • Meet at a gate, not at "Shinjuku Station." The station is huge. Agree on a gate name, such as "JR New South Gate," and you'll find each other.
  • Follow your company's color and name on the signs. The signs guide you by line and company, and that's quicker than looking for platform numbers.
  • Expect some changes. JR East is carrying out station improvement work. In December 2025, for example, it closed part of Chuo Passage 1 and moved the stairs to platforms 11–12 to Passage 2. Follow the signs on the day.
  • Ask staff at a staffed gate if your ticket or card won't open the gate. It's much quicker than trying the next one.
