Onsen Towns You Can Reach by Train from Tokyo: Hakone, Atami, Kinugawa and Kusatsu

Day trip guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: for a day trip, Hakone and Atami are the easy picks. The Romancecar gets you from Shinjuku to Hakone-Yumoto in about 80 minutes, and the shinkansen reaches Atami in around 50 minutes from Tokyo. Kinugawa Onsen is about 1 hour 50 minutes from Asakusa on Tobu's direct limited express, so it works as a day trip or pairs well with Nikko. Kusatsu is the furthest, more than two and a half hours by train and bus, so it's best with a night there.

In Kusatsu, the public baths are open to day visitors, so you don't need a ryokan booking to get in the water there. Below are the official train options, a few prices, and the bath manners that matter most. Prices are at the time of writing (November 2026) and can change.

Hakone: the all-rounder

Odakyu's Romancecar limited express runs from Shinjuku to Hakone-Yumoto. Odakyu says the trip "takes about 80 minutes", against about 2 hours on regular express trains. If you're starting from Shinjuku, our Shinjuku Station guide helps you find the Odakyu platforms.

  • Romancecar fee: "All seats are reserved." The limited express fee from Shinjuku to Hakone-Yumoto is 1,200 yen for adults and 600 yen for children (one way), paid on top of your fare, pass or IC card. Tickets go on sale at 10 a.m. Japan time one month before departure, and Odakyu takes 50 yen off if you buy the e-ticket online.
  • Hakone Freepass: from Shinjuku it costs 7,100 yen (2 days) or 7,500 yen (3 days) for adults, and includes one round trip on the Odakyu Line between Shinjuku and Odawara plus unlimited rides on Hakone's mountain railway, cable car, ropeway, sightseeing cruise and the buses in the designated area. Odakyu notes that "a separate Limited Express Romancecar ticket is required," and that "2 days does not mean 48 hours": the 2-day pass ends after the last service on the following day. The pass also gives "discounts on entrance fees, etc., at around 70 tourist spots."

If you'd rather have it on your phone before you travel, the Hakone Freepass on Klook (affiliate link) was rated 4.6 from about 11,700 reviews and showed from US$44.95 on Klook at the time of writing; prices vary by option. For whether a pass beats single tickets on your route, see which rail passes are worth it.

Luggage: for a one-night stay, the Hakone Luggage Transport Service at Hakone-Yumoto Station takes your bag to your hotel. It costs 1,500 yen per piece for a regular bag (up to 140 cm total, 15 kg) and 2,500 yen for a large one (up to 200 cm, 30 kg). Drop-off is 8:30–12:00 and it's ready at your hotel "after 15:00". On the way back you hand it in at the hotel front desk "until 10:00" and pick it up at the station 13:30–18:30. It only serves partner hotels, so check that yours is on the list. For a day trip, Odakyu's baggage storage at Hakone-Yumoto takes bags 9:00–17:30 (up to 160 cm and 25 kg). For the wider area, including Lake Ashi and Mt. Fuji views, see our Mt. Fuji and Hakone day trip guide.

Atami: the fastest from Tokyo

The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) calls Atami "one of Japan's leading onsen resorts". From Tokyo you can take the Kodama on the Tokaido Shinkansen or the limited express Odoriko. JNTO says "the shinkansen is the fastest at around 50 minutes." JR East says the Odoriko from Tokyo Station to Atami takes 1 hour 20 minutes, and it is "a limited express train with reserved seats." Highway buses also run, but JNTO puts them at "two and a half to three hours depending on traffic."

If you take the shinkansen, our Tokyo Station guide and how to ride the shinkansen cover the gates and seats. One thing to know if you plan to continue towards Shuzenji on a JR East pass: JR East says "the passes offered by JR-EAST cannot be used between Atami Station and Shuzenji Station."

Kinugawa Onsen: a good pair with Nikko

Kinugawa Onsen is reached on Tobu Railway trains from Asakusa. Tobu says the trip from Asakusa to Kinugawa-Onsen on the Limited Express SPACIA takes "approximately 1 hour 50 minutes (direct train)", and JNTO says regular trains take "about two and a half hours." There are also direct through trains from JR Shinjuku and Ikebukuro, which Tobu puts at about 2 hours or a little more.

  • Tickets: Tobu says "two tickets are required per passenger to ride a limited express train". Tobu's timetable notes you can't board the JR through trains (Nikko, Kinugawa, SPACIA Kinugawa) with PASMO or Suica alone, so buy those tickets in advance. Tobu's online booking doesn't cover these JR through trains.
  • NIKKO PASS all area: 8,000 yen for adults, valid 1–4 consecutive days, and its area includes Kinugawa Onsen. There is also a cheaper world heritage area version (3,000 yen) with a smaller area. Tobu says "this pass does not include a limited express ticket," and the through route from JR Shinjuku and Ikebukuro "is not covered by Nikko pass."

Because the NIKKO PASS all area covers Kinugawa Onsen too, it's easy to combine the two. See our Nikko day trip guide for the Tobu trains and passes in more detail.

Kusatsu Onsen: worth a night

Kusatsu is in Gunma Prefecture. According to the Kusatsu Tourism Association (data as of October 2025), the limited express Kusatsu-Shima takes 2 hours 18 minutes from Ueno to Naganohara-Kusatsuguchi, and the JR bus from there to Kusatsu Onsen takes about 25 minutes (780 yen for adults). The association also lists a highway bus from Tokyo Station or Busta Shinjuku at 3,700–4,100 yen for adults, depending on the date.

In the middle of town is the Yubatake, which the association calls "the symbol of Kusatsu Onsen": about 4,000 liters of hot water gush out of it every minute. A few things you can do without a ryokan booking:

  • Sainokawara Rotenburo (an outdoor bath): 800 yen for adults. Open 7:00–20:00 from April 1 to November 30 and 9:00–20:00 from December 1 to March 31, with last entry at 19:30. The town says Fridays are a "mixed-bathing day" for couples, friends and families.
  • Otakinoyu: 1,200 yen for adults, open 9:00–21:00 with last entry at 20:00. The town notes there are maintenance closure days in December, so check before you go.
  • Yumomi show at Netsunoyu: 700 yen for adults; tickets go on sale 30 minutes before each show.

The association gives the Yubatake source's pH as 2.1, so the water is strongly acidic.

Onsen manners in two minutes

Japanese baths have a few rules. These come from JNTO's bathing guides:

  • Wash first. "You are expected to thoroughly wash your whole body before entering the hot spring." Use the soap and shampoo at the washing stations and rinse off completely.
  • Leave swimwear in the changing room. "Onsen bathing is typically enjoyed in the nude, so clothing (including swimwear) should be left in the changing area."
  • Keep the small towel out of the water. Fold it and put it on your head or on the side of the bath. Tie up long hair or wrap it in a towel.
  • Tattoos: "Some traditional onsen may not accept people with a tattoo, but this is beginning to change." JNTO calls it "very case-by-case" and suggests you "email the facility directly and ask what their rules are before you go." A private bath (kashikiri) that you rent for your group is another option.
  • No photos in the bathing area, and avoid alcohol before bathing.

Practical tips from the railway side

  • Book the limited express ahead. On the Romancecar "all seats are reserved", and its tickets go on sale one month before departure.
  • An IC card alone isn't enough for some limited expresses. The Romancecar fee is paid on top of an IC card, and Tobu says you can't board the JR through trains to Kinugawa with PASMO or Suica alone. See our IC card guide.
  • Travel light for one night. Send your big suitcase on to your next hotel with luggage forwarding, or leave it in a station locker, and take a small overnight bag to the onsen.
