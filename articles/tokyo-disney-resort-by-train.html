Tokyo Disney Resort by Train: Maihama Station, the Disney Resort Line, and the Airports

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: get off at JR Maihama Station on the Keiyo Line. From Tokyo Station it is about 20 minutes with no change. Tokyo Disneyland is about a five-minute walk from the station; Tokyo DisneySea is a short ride on the Disney Resort Line monorail. And buy your park ticket online before you go — the parks do not sell tickets at the gate.

Tokyo Disney Resort sits in Urayasu, Chiba, just outside Tokyo — close enough for a day trip, far enough that the wrong route costs you an hour. The resort asks guests to use public transport, because the roads and car parks around it jam badly. The train route is simple once you know the one station name that matters: Maihama.

From Tokyo Station: the Keiyo Line

Take the JR Keiyo Line (or a Musashino Line through-train) from Tokyo Station to Maihama. Tokyo Disney Resort's own directions put the trip at about 20 minutes in total, with about 15 minutes on the train and no transfer. The fare is ¥253 with an IC card (¥260 for a paper ticket) at the time of writing, after JR East's fare revision in March 2026. A Japan Rail Pass covers the ride, as does any JR East pass that includes the Tokyo area.

The catch is inside Tokyo Station itself. The Keiyo Line platforms sit a long way from the other JR lines and the shinkansen, down at the far end of the station near the Yaesu side. Follow the signs for the Keiyo Line (JE) and allow about 10 minutes for the walk — the total trip from a shinkansen platform to the Maihama gates is closer to 35 or 40 minutes than 20. Coming from elsewhere in Tokyo? Our guide to Tokyo's train network explains the transfer, and our Tokyo Station guide covers the two sides of the station.

On the platform: any local or rapid Keiyo Line or Musashino Line train that lists Maihama as a stop will take you there, but the limited expresses — Wakashio and Sazanami — skip Maihama altogether. If the departure board shows one of those names, wait for the next train.

From Narita and Haneda airports

Both airports connect to Maihama by train, and both also have direct shuttle buses. The resort's own times leave out the time you spend changing trains, so treat them as the moving time, not the door-to-door time.

The train is usually faster and never gets stuck in traffic, but the bus wins with big suitcases: it drops you at the park bus terminals with no stairs or transfers. The Disney Resort Line monorail

From Maihama Station, the resort's own railway takes over. The Disney Resort Line is a monorail loop run by the Maihama Resort Line, and it circles four stations in about 13 minutes: Resort Gateway, Tokyo Disneyland, Bayside and Tokyo DisneySea, in that order. Trains run from 6:03 a.m. to 11:30 p.m., and the first and last trains of the day both leave from Resort Gateway Station.

Resort Gateway is the station by JR Maihama: leave by the South Exit, turn left, about a two-minute walk. Which station you need depends on where you are going:

  • Tokyo Disneyland Station stands in front of Disneyland's main entrance. But you probably do not need it — Disneyland is about a five-minute walk from JR Maihama Station, so walking is quicker than riding the loop round.
  • Bayside Station is the stop for the resort's Official Hotels. The six Official Hotels run a free shuttle bus, the Disney Resort Cruiser, to Bayside Station.
  • Tokyo DisneySea Station stands in front of DisneySea's main entrance, about nine minutes by monorail from Resort Gateway, plus about a minute's walk at each end.

The monorail is a separate railway with its own fare — your park ticket does not cover it. A single ticket is good for up to one full loop on the day you buy it, and Day Passes give unlimited rides for the number of days you buy.

Tickets and Day Passes are sold at machines inside every station, and you can pay with Suica, PASMO, ICOCA and other major IC cards. Up to two children under 6 ride free with each paying adult. The maths is simple: a DisneySea return is two single tickets (¥600), so the ¥700 1-Day Pass only pays off if you ride three times or more — for example, out to DisneySea, back to the hotels at Bayside, and out again.

Buy your park ticket before you go

A 1-Day Passport is for one park on one date, and the price changes by date. Adult tickets start at ¥8,900 at the time of writing; on the late-2026 calendars, a weekday cost ¥10,900, a Sunday ¥11,900 and a Saturday ¥12,400. Junior (12–17) and child (4–11) tickets start at ¥7,400 and ¥5,300, and children aged 3 and under go in free.

Tickets go on sale two months ahead at 14:00 Japan time, each day for the same date two months later, and they are sold on the official website and in the Tokyo Disney Resort App only — the ticket booths at the parks do not sell them. You need a free MyDisney account to buy, and each ticket names its park and date. The date can be changed free of charge if the new date is not sold out, but the park cannot. Popular dates do sell out, so book as soon as your date goes on sale. If the official site has no tickets left for your date, travel agencies also sell them: the Tokyo Disney Resort 1-Day Passport on Klook (affiliate link) is rated 4.8 from more than 100,000 reviews, with 6M+ booked, from about US$58.80 at the time of writing.

A few things railway staff wish visitors knew

  • One IC card does the whole trip. Your Suica or PASMO covers the JR ride to Maihama and the monorail, so there is no need to buy separate paper tickets. See our IC card guide if you do not have one yet.
  • Don't plan a tight connection at Tokyo Station. That 10-minute walk to the Keiyo Line platforms catches people out, especially with suitcases. Build it into your plan.
  • Check the last train home before the night show. The monorail's last train leaves Resort Gateway Station at 11:30 p.m., and the Keiyo Line back to Tokyo runs late — but "late" is not "all night", so check the time before you settle in for the fireworks.
  • Travel light if you can. Maihama Station and the monorail get very crowded at opening time. If you are heading to a hotel afterwards, consider sending the big suitcase ahead and taking just a day bag to the parks.
  • Staying at an Official Hotel? Use the free Disney Resort Cruiser shuttle bus to Bayside Station instead of walking back to Maihama — it runs between the hotels and the monorail.

The train part of a Disney day is the easy part. One station, one line from Tokyo, one short monorail ride for DisneySea — and a ticket bought before you leave home.
