eSIM, Pocket WiFi, and Portable Chargers in Japan: What Visitors Actually Need

Connectivity guide · Updated with current provider comparisons

Before you can use any map, train app, or translation tool in Japan, you need working mobile data — and before you can keep using your phone all day for maps and photos, you'll probably need a way to charge it. Here's a straight comparison of your options, without the padded "top 10" lists most sites run.

eSIM: the easiest option if your phone supports it

If your phone is eSIM-compatible and unlocked, this is generally the least hassle: no physical SIM swap, no airport counter, and you can often activate it before you land. A few well-known options as of late 2026:

  • Airalo — one of the most widely used eSIM marketplaces for travelers; a 7-day unlimited plan runs around $26.50. Also useful if your trip continues on to South Korea or Taiwan, since it sells regional plans covering multiple countries.
  • Ubigi — runs on the NTT docomo network, which tends to have the best coverage in rural and mountainous areas. A 10GB/7-day plan is around $14, and it lets you top up data from inside its own app.
  • Japan Wireless eSIM — runs on KDDI (5G plans) or IIJmio (4G plans), with Japan-based customer support by phone, chat, and email. If the eSIM fails to connect, they'll ship a free pocket WiFi to your accommodation as a backup — useful if you're nervous about relying entirely on an eSIM.

None of these come with an actual Japanese phone number — they're data-only, which is fine for maps, messaging apps, and translation tools, but won't let you receive SMS verification codes from Japanese services.

Pocket WiFi: better if you're traveling with others or need a backup number-free option

A rental pocket WiFi router gives you a single hotspot that multiple devices (and multiple people) can connect to at once, which makes it a better value than individual eSIMs if you're traveling as a group or family. It also doesn't require your phone to support eSIM at all, so it works for older phones. The trade-off is you have to carry, charge, and return the device — eSIM has none of that hassle for solo travelers.

One option is an unlimited-data pocket WiFi that you pick up at the airport when you land, which comes with a free power bank. It was rated 4.7 from about 11,700 reviews on Klook at the time of writing: Japan pocket WiFi on Klook (affiliate link). Check which airport counter and opening hours apply to your flight.

Portable chargers: you don't need to bring your own

Japan has an extensive network of rental power bank kiosks — services like ChargeSPOT are everywhere: convenience stores, train stations, shopping centers. You tap your phone or scan a QR code to unlock a battery, and you can return it to any other kiosk in the network, not just the one you rented from. For a day of heavy map use and photography, it's a convenient backup that saves you from carrying your own power bank through airport security.

So which should you actually get?

SituationBest fit
Solo traveler, eSIM-compatible phone, light-to-moderate useeSIM (Japan Wireless or Ubigi for short trips)
Heavy data use — lots of video, photo uploadseSIM unlimited plan, or pocket WiFi
Traveling as a group or familyPocket WiFi (one router, shared by everyone)
Older phone without eSIM supportPocket WiFi, or a physical prepaid SIM
Battery running low mid-dayA rental power bank kiosk (ChargeSPOT etc.), no need to bring your own
