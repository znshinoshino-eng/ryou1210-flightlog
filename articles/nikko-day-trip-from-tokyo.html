Nikko Day Trip from Tokyo: Trains, Passes and What to See

Day-trip guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: The simplest way to Nikko is the Tobu limited express from Asakusa, which takes about 1 hour 50 minutes to Tobu-Nikko with every seat reserved. If you hold a Japan Rail Pass, take the shinkansen to Utsunomiya and change to the JR Nikko Line instead. For a day or two around the shrines, Tobu's NIKKO PASS World Heritage Area (¥3,000 for adults at the time of writing) covers the train from Asakusa and the local buses, but not the limited express seat.

Three ways to get from Tokyo to Nikko

Nikko has two railway stations: Tobu-Nikko (Tobu Railway) and JR Nikko. Which one you arrive at depends on the route you pick.

1. Tobu limited express from Asakusa or Tokyo Skytree

Tobu runs limited expresses from Asakusa and Tokyo Skytree stations. Trains to Tobu-Nikko are called Kegon (the Kinu trains go to Kinugawa-Onsen instead). Tobu gives about 1 hour 50 minutes for a direct train to Tobu-Nikko. Some trains need a change at Shimo-Imaichi, so check the timetable when you book.

All seats on Tobu limited expresses are reserved, so you need a limited express ticket on top of the normal fare. On the newest train, SPACIA X, the Standard Seat charge from Asakusa to Tobu-Nikko is ¥1,940 on regular days (¥2,140 on peak days, ¥1,740 on off-peak days), plus the base fare, at the time of writing. Premium Seats are ¥2,520 on regular days. Children pay half the limited express charge.

You can book online from 9:00 am Japan time one month before you travel, up to 3 minutes before departure, or buy at ticket machines at major Tobu stations. Buying on board or at the train door costs an extra ¥200, so it pays to buy first.

Don't want to pay the express charge? Local trains also run, but JNTO says they take at least two and a half hours with up to three changes.

2. JR–Tobu through limited express from Shinjuku

JR East and Tobu run direct limited expresses from Shinjuku, stopping at Ikebukuro and Omiya. Trains named Nikko and SPACIA Nikko go to Tobu-Nikko, while Kinugawa and SPACIA Kinugawa go to Kinugawa-Onsen. The ride takes about 2 hours. This is handy if you're staying on the west side of Tokyo (see our Shinjuku Station guide).

Two catches. The Japan Rail Pass covers these trains only on the JR part of the route: from Kurihashi onward the line belongs to Tobu, and you pay that section separately. And Tobu says this route is not covered by the NIKKO PASS.

3. Shinkansen and the JR Nikko Line

Take the Tohoku Shinkansen from Tokyo or Ueno to Utsunomiya, then the JR Nikko Line to JR Nikko Station. JNTO gives around 1 hour 40 minutes in total. It costs more than the Tobu train and you have to change, but it is fully covered by the Japan Rail Pass. That makes it the obvious choice for pass holders. New to the shinkansen? Which pass, if any?

Tobu sells two passes that only foreign travelers can buy (Japanese nationals travelling with them can buy one too). You start the trip at Asakusa or Tokyo Skytree station.

  • NIKKO PASS World Heritage Area: ¥3,000 (child ¥1,500), 2 days. It includes one round trip between Asakusa and Shimo-Imaichi, Tobu trains between Shimo-Imaichi, Tobu-Nikko and Shin-Fujiwara, and Tobu buses inside the World Heritage area around the shrines.
  • NIKKO PASS All Area: ¥8,000 (child ¥4,000), 4 days. It covers the same trains plus all Tobu buses in Nikko, including Lake Chuzenji and Yumoto. In season it also covers the Lake Chuzenji sightseeing cruise and the Odashirogahara hybrid bus (mid-April to 30 November).

Prices are at the time of writing. Neither pass includes the limited express ticket: without one you ride local trains, so add a SPACIA or Revaty seat when you buy (online or at the tourist counter). Shrine and temple admission is not included either. You can buy a digital pass online, or buy in person at the TOBU Tourist Information Center in Asakusa from one month before travel.

If you're coming from Shinjuku, look at the JR TOKYO Wide Pass instead. It costs ¥16,000 (child ¥8,000) for 3 consecutive days at the time of writing. It covers reserved ordinary seats on the Nikko, Kinugawa and SPACIA Kinugawa through trains, and Tobu local trains between Shimo-Imaichi and Tobu-Nikko. Limited expresses that start or end at Tobu stations (such as those from Asakusa) are not covered. It only pays off if you use it for other trips in those 3 days too. Getting around Nikko

From either station, the World Heritage Meguri bus loops past the main sights. It takes about 10 minutes to the Omotesando stop, then about 5 minutes on foot to Toshogu. Tobu Bus sells a one-day World Heritage Meguri Tegata pass for ¥600 (child ¥300), valid only on the day you buy it. Paper passes are sold at the Tobu-Nikko Station tourist center, and Tobu Bus also sells passes through mobile apps.

You can also walk. Nikko's tourism association puts the Shinkyo Bridge about 1.5 km from JR Nikko Station, roughly a 20–30 minute walk.

For Lake Chuzenji and Kegon Falls, take the Tobu bus towards Chuzenji Onsen. A warning for autumn: during the autumn leaves (roughly mid-October to mid-November), visitnikko.jp says the usual 45-minute drive from Nikko Station to the lake can take over 4 hours, and buses use the same road. In that season, go up very early or leave the lake for another trip. Tobu Bus also sells a 2-day Chuzenji Onsen Free Pass for ¥2,300 (child ¥1,150).

Fees are at the time of writing. Last entry is 30 minutes before closing at Toshogu and Futarasan, and 20 minutes before closing at the Kegon Falls elevator (which can also stop in bad weather). For the Rinnoji Sanbutsudo + Taiyuin ticket, the last entry is earlier: 14:30 (Apr–Oct) and 13:30 (Nov–Mar).

  • Toshogu is the reason most people come. Elementary and junior high students pay ¥550, and a set ticket with the Treasure House is ¥2,400 for adults. Give it at least an hour or two.
  • Rinnoji covers two sites: the Sanbutsudo hall and Taiyuin. The combined ticket is cheaper than buying them separately (¥400 + ¥550).
  • Futarasan Shrine is the third of the main sites. The ¥500 fee is for the Honsha shrine garden.
  • Shinkyo Bridge is the red bridge in the photo above. The ¥300 fee is for walking onto it, and the ticket counter takes cash only.

A simple one-day plan

  1. Morning: An early Tobu limited express from Asakusa (or shinkansen to Utsunomiya with a JR Pass). Book your seat in advance, especially for weekends and autumn.
  2. Late morning: Bus or walk to Shinkyo Bridge, then up to Toshogu before the biggest crowds.
  3. Early afternoon: Rinnoji (Sanbutsudo and Taiyuin) before the combined ticket's last entry, then Futarasan Shrine. Walk or take the bus back to the station.
  4. Optional: With an early start and outside the autumn-leaf rush, go on to Kegon Falls and Lake Chuzenji. Otherwise, save them for a second day.

Remember that closing times drop to 16:00 at Toshogu from 1 November, so in winter the shrine visit has to come first. If you're carrying luggage, see our guide to coin lockers and luggage storage at stations. For more easy trips out of Tokyo, try Kamakura and Enoshima or Mt. Fuji and Hakone.
