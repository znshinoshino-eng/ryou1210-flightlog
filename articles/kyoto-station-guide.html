Kyoto Station Guide: Exits, the Bus Terminal, Trains and Luggage

Station guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: Kyoto Station has two main sides. The Central Exit (north, facing Kyoto Tower) is where the city bus terminal is. The Hachijo Exit (south) is the shinkansen side, with expressway and airport buses. Inside, you'll find four railways: JR (shinkansen and local lines), the Karasuma subway line and the Kintetsu line to Nara. Pick the one that matches where you're going, and you'll often skip the crowded buses completely.

Kyoto Station is big, and the different railways are run by different companies, so the signs can feel confusing on your first visit. Everything below comes from the Kyoto City Tourism Association, the Kyoto City Bus and Subway website, and the operators' own pages.

Central Exit vs Hachijo Exit

Almost everything at Kyoto Station is described by which side it's on. Learn these two names and the rest gets easier.

SideWhat's there
Central Exit (north, Karasuma side)Kyoto Tower across the road, the city bus terminal, the taxi stand in front of Hotel Granvia, and the JR side of the station
Hachijo Exit (south)The shinkansen exits, expressway and airport buses, hotel shuttle buses, and another taxi stand

One thing that confuses visitors: the shinkansen has its own Shinkansen Central Exit, and despite the name, it leads to the Hachijo (south) side, not to the Central Exit with Kyoto Tower. If you get off the shinkansen and want the city buses, follow the signs for the JR transfer gates or walk through the station to the Central Exit.

The Kyoto Tourist Information Center (Kyo-Navi) is on the 2nd floor of the Kyoto Station Building, in the north–south passageway outside the ticket gates. It's open 8:30 to 19:00, according to the Kyoto City Bus guide map.

JR, subway or Kintetsu: which one do you need?

Kyoto Station is the main hub for every railway in the city. Kyoto's own tourism site points out that trains are usually more reliable than the city bus, which can be packed and slowed by traffic. Here's how the four rail options split up:

RailwayUse it forWhere to find it
JR ShinkansenTokyo, Nagoya, Shin-Osaka, HiroshimaShinkansen gates; exits lead to the Hachijo side
JR conventional linesOsaka, Fushimi Inari, Arashiyama, Uji, Nara, Kansai AirportJR side, through the Central Exit
Karasuma subway lineCentral Kyoto, north–south; change at Karasuma-Oike for the Tozai Line (Nijo-jo Castle, Heian-jingu Shrine)Underground
Kintetsu Kyoto LineTo-ji Temple (one stop) and NaraVia the Shinkansen Central Exit, or the JR West Exit

A few practical notes:

  • Getting to Kintetsu. From the shinkansen concourse, use the Shinkansen Central Exit. From the JR lines, take the West Exit and turn left.
  • Getting to the subway with luggage. The Kyoto City Tourism Association recommends going via the Underground East Exit if you have big bags. The routes from the Hachijo side are narrower, with few escalators or elevators.
  • Kintetsu to Nara is about 35 minutes by limited express (extra fee). JR passes don't cover Kintetsu. Our Nara day trip guide compares it with JR.
  • A JR tip: if your JR ticket's destination is inside Kyoto City, you can get off at any JR conventional line station in the city at no extra charge.

Paying is easy on all four: IC cards work on JR, the subway, Kintetsu and the city bus. See our guide to Suica, PASMO and ICOCA.

  • The Sagano Line platforms (31–33) are the farthest away. They're beyond a 558-metre platform that the Kyoto City Tourism Association calls the longest in Japan, so allow extra time for Arashiyama trains.
  • From the shinkansen to JR lines, you don't need to go outside. Use the Central Transfer gate, which has escalators and elevators and leads to every JR line. The East Transfer gate is handy for the Kyoto, Kosei and Nara lines.

The bus terminal (Central Exit)

The city bus terminal is directly in front of the Central Exit, at the foot of Kyoto Tower, under a curved white roof. It's split into lettered boarding areas, and the signs and the Bus Information Center next to the stops tell you which one serves your route. The Hachijo side also has bus stops, but those are mostly expressway, airport and hotel shuttle buses.

  • Fares: inside the central flat-fare area, a ride is ¥230 for adults and ¥120 for children at the time of writing (October 2026).
  • Day pass: the Subway & Bus 1-Day Pass is ¥1,100 for adults and ¥550 for children, sold at Kyo-Navi, the Bus Information Center and ticket machines at bus stops and subway stations.
  • Sightseeing limited express buses: the EX100 runs from stop D1 to Gojozaka (for Kiyomizu-dera), Gion, Okazaki/Heian-jingu and Ginkakuji-mae, and the EX101 runs from D1 straight to Gojozaka. The city gives about 12 minutes to Gojozaka, 17 to Gion and 30 to Ginkakuji-mae. The fare is ¥500 for adults and ¥250 for children (cash or IC card), and the Subway & Bus 1-Day Pass covers it at no extra charge. They don't run every day, so check the Kyoto City Bus website for operating days before you plan around them.
  • Big suitcases: the city warns that drivers can refuse luggage that's too large, and asks passengers not to wear backpacks on board. Leave your suitcase at the station or hotel (see below) before getting on a bus.

Taxis are another option with luggage. The Central Exit taxi stand is in front of Hotel Granvia, and the Hachijo Exit stand is on the south side.

Coin lockers, luggage storage and delivery

Lockers at Kyoto Station can fill up at busy times. The city's official HANDS FREE KYOTO site has maps of the station's coin lockers, staffed storage counters and luggage delivery points, so check it before you arrive. A few options from operators' own pages:

  • SPACER cashless lockers (JR West): outside the Central Gate, turn left after you exit. Available 4:35 to 23:55. You can check free lockers in real time and reserve one online.
  • Karasuma-side baggage counter (Sagawa, in JTB Kyoto Ekimae, 1F): open 10:00 to 18:00. Storage is ¥1,000 per bag per day up to 160 cm (length + width + height) and ¥1,500 above that. Same-day delivery to partner hotels in Kyoto City is ¥1,000 per bag (up to 140 cm and 20 kg). Drop off by 14:00, and it arrives after 17:00.
  • JR Kyoto Station Shinkansen Hachijo Delivery Service: right outside the Shinkansen Hachijo East gate, open 8:45 to 19:30 every day. It stores bags and delivers between the station and hotels in Kyoto City. Bags left between 9:00 and 14:00 reach your hotel by 17:00.

The simple plan on arrival day: hand your suitcase in near the station, sightsee with a light bag, and collect it at your hotel in the evening. Moving between cities? Luggage forwarding to your next hotel saves dragging bags through the station at all.

Kansai Airport from Kyoto Station

For Kansai Airport, the JR Haruka limited express runs straight from Kyoto Station in about 75 minutes, from platforms 6, 7 or 30. JR passes such as the JR Kansai Area Pass cover it. If you don't have a pass, a JR Haruka ticket on Klook (affiliate link) is rated 4.8 from about 47,000 reviews, from US$7.70 on Klook at the time of writing (prices vary by route and option). Tips from the railway side

  • Say "Central Exit" or "Hachijo Exit," not just "Kyoto Station." The two sides are a long walk apart, especially with luggage.
  • Try a train before the bus. Fushimi Inari, Arashiyama, Nara and To-ji all have rail access from this station, and trains don't get stuck in traffic.
  • Allow time for Arashiyama and airport trains. Platforms 30–33 are at the far end of the station.
  • Ask at a staffed gate if your ticket or IC card won't open a gate. It's faster than trying other gates.
