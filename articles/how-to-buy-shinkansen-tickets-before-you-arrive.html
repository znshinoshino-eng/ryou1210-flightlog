How to Buy Shinkansen Tickets Before You Arrive

Rail guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: book the Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu Shinkansen on SmartEX, the lines north of Tokyo on JR-EAST Train Reservation, and western Japan's limited expresses on JR West's e5489. Seats go on sale at 10:00 Japan time, one month before travel — except SmartEX, which takes requests up to a year ahead. Outside the big holidays you can also just buy at the station, but a seat booked from home costs less than a paper ticket and saves you the queue.

There is no single place to buy shinkansen tickets. Japan's railway companies each run their own booking site, and each site only sells its own lines — pick the wrong one and your route simply will not appear. This guide sorts out which site is yours. It also covers when seats go on sale, and what the main routes cost.

If your trip crosses from one company's lines to another — Tokyo to Kanazawa, for example — start with the site for the line you board first. Either of the first two sites sells the Hokuriku Shinkansen as far as Tsuruga.

SmartEX: the one most visitors need

SmartEX is the official booking site of JR Central, JR West and JR Kyushu, and it covers the lines most first trips use: the Tokaido Shinkansen from Tokyo to Kyoto and Osaka, the Sanyo Shinkansen on to Hiroshima and Hakata, and the Kyushu Shinkansen down to Kagoshima-Chuo. It works in English, membership is free, and you need to be 18 or over with a credit card. The card must support 3-D Secure, the extra check your bank runs when you pay online.

Booking opens at 5:30 in the morning, one year before your travel date, and stays open until four minutes before the train leaves. There is a catch to the one-year window: more than a month out you are choosing from a provisional timetable, and SmartEX confirms your exact train, time and seat after 8:00 in the morning, one month before you travel, and tells you by email. To pick your exact train and seat from the map, book from 10:00 Japan time, one month before, when ordinary sales open.

You board with a QR ticket on your phone, or by linking an IC card such as Suica and tapping through the gate. One booking covers one to six people. Changes are free as often as you like before departure, and a refund before departure costs ¥320 per ticket.

If your date is fixed three weeks or more ahead, check the early-bird fare first. EX Hayatoku 21 gives you a reserved seat on a Nozomi for ¥12,430 between Tokyo and Kyoto — ¥1,540 less than the standard SmartEX fare — if you book by 11:30 p.m., 21 days before travel. Seats are limited, it is not sold around Golden Week, Obon or the New Year holiday, and you cannot change it afterwards, only refund it and book again. A standard SmartEX seat on the same train is ¥13,970 — ¥200 less than the ¥14,170 paper ticket — and it keeps the free changes.

JR-EAST Train Reservation: trains north of Tokyo

For the lines north and west of Tokyo — Tohoku, Hokkaido, Akita, Yamagata, Joetsu and Hokuriku — the booking site is JR-EAST Train Reservation, the English version of JR East's Eki-net. Membership is free, and as with SmartEX you need a credit card that supports 3-D Secure. Seats go on sale at 10:00 Japan time, one month before travel. There is no one-year window here.

The cheapest way to ride is the Shinkansen e-ticket: you book a reserved seat, link your IC card — Suica, PASMO, ICOCA and the other major cards all work — and tap through the shinkansen gate with no paper ticket at all. An e-ticket costs ¥200 less than a paper ticket for a reserved seat. As an example, Tokyo to Sendai on a Hayabusa is ¥11,430 as an e-ticket for one adult, one way, in the regular season, at the time of writing. JR East also runs occasional limited-period discount sales on Eki-net, so check the site before you pay the full fare — the old year-round discount tickets are gone.

One thing the English site cannot do: reserve seats for the nationwide Japan Rail Pass — pass holders book those free at a station machine or ticket office. If you are still deciding between a pass and single tickets, see our regional passes guide and which rail passes are worth it.

JR West e5489: the west, and the airport expresses

JR West's site is called e5489, or JR-WEST Online Train Reservation. It sells the Sanyo and Kyushu Shinkansen, the Hokuriku Shinkansen, and JR West's limited expresses — the Haruka from Kansai Airport to Kyoto and Osaka, the Thunderbird to Kanazawa, and others. Reservations open at 10:00 Japan time, one month before travel, and stay open until six minutes before departure. You do not need to register as a member to use it.

Unlike SmartEX, e5489 issues paper tickets: you collect them from a ticket machine at a station in western Japan, using your booking number and the card you paid with. Allow time for that on your first day.

Buying through Klook instead

If you would rather pay in your own currency, Klook sells shinkansen e-tickets for the two routes most visitors ride: Tokyo to Kyoto shinkansen on Klook (affiliate link), from US$85.30 at the time of writing, and Tokyo to Osaka shinkansen on Klook (affiliate link), from US$88.49. You get a QR code to board with, the same as SmartEX. Check how the ticket is issued on the listing, and compare the price in yen, since Klook's price moves with the exchange rate.

Or just buy at the station

At a major station, the reserved-seat ticket machines have English menus and sell shinkansen tickets with seat reservations — look for the machines marked for shinkansen or limited express tickets. For a rail pass, an unusual route or a question, go to the staffed ticket office instead, with your route and preferred time ready.

On an ordinary day, a non-reserved seat on the next train is almost always available: buy the ticket from a machine, walk through the gate and sit in cars 1 to 3. But during the big holidays — the New Year, Golden Week and Obon — every Nozomi runs with reserved seats only, and the unreserved cars disappear. Book ahead for those dates, or take a Hikari or Kodama instead. One more buying decision: if your suitcase measures more than 160 cm in total (length + width + height), reserve a seat with an oversized-baggage area — it is compulsory on the Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu Shinkansen, and riding without one costs a ¥1,000 fee. See our shinkansen guide for how that works.

Reserved-seat prices move with the season: ¥200 less on off-peak days, ¥200 more in peak season and ¥400 more in the busiest season. Non-reserved seats do not change. The Nozomi takes about 2 hours 15 minutes; the Hikari about 2 hours 40 minutes. One note for 2027: JR Central is raising Nozomi reserved fares for tickets bought from 1 July 2027 — Tokyo to Kyoto goes up by ¥700.

A few things that catch people out

  • Your card is the usual failure point. SmartEX and Eki-net both need a card with 3-D Secure, and debit and prepaid cards often fail. If your card is declined, try another credit card — or buy at the station.
  • SmartEX and the Japan Rail Pass do not mix. You cannot book a JR Pass seat on SmartEX. Pass holders reserve free at a station machine or ticket office.
  • A reserved seat is for one train. If you miss it on a standard SmartEX fare, you can still take a later train the same day in a non-reserved seat. A Hayatoku ticket is only valid on the train you booked.
  • Book the peak dates at 10:00, one month out. For the New Year, Golden Week and Obon, the popular morning trains sell out fast. Set an alarm for 10:00 Japan time on the day sales open — or put in a SmartEX request a year ahead.
  • Keep an IC card for the rest of the trip. Shinkansen tickets cover the shinkansen only. Subways, buses and local trains need an IC card or a separate ticket — see our IC card guide.

Buy the ticket on the right site, a month ahead, and the shinkansen becomes the easy part of the trip. The hard part is deciding which side to sit on for the Fuji view — seat E, on the right heading west from Tokyo, on a clear day.
