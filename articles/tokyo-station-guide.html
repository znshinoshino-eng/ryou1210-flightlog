Tokyo Station Guide: Gates, Platforms and Shinkansen Transfers

Station guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: Tokyo Station has two sides, Marunouchi and Yaesu, and a dozen gates between them. Before you go, check three things: which side your meeting point, hotel or bus is on, which platform your train leaves from, and how long the transfer really takes. JR East's own guideline is 10 minutes from the shinkansen to the Yamanote Line, but 15 minutes to the Narita Airport trains and 20 minutes to the Keiyo Line for Disney.

At big stations like this, visitors often stop staff to ask the way. The station is huge, but it gets much easier once you know how it's laid out. Everything below comes from JR East's station pages and the official Tokyo Station City site.

Marunouchi side vs Yaesu side

Gates are named after the side they open onto. If someone says "meet me at the Yaesu Central Gate," that tells you both the side and the gate.

SideGround floor (1F) gatesUnderground (B1) gates
MarunouchiNorth, Central, SouthMarunouchi Underground North, Marunouchi Underground South
YaesuNorth, Central, SouthYaesu Underground Central
OtherNihombashi Gate, Central Passage

The Marunouchi side is home to the red-brick Marunouchi Station Building, the one in the photo above. A few things worth knowing by side:

  • Yaesu South Gate: JR Expressway Buses leave directly in front of this gate. The Bus Terminal Tokyo Yaesu is on floors B1 and B2 of Tokyo Midtown Yaesu.
  • Marunouchi South Gate: for Hato Bus sightseeing tours, exit here and turn left.
  • Marunouchi North Gate: the JR EAST Travel Service Center is here, outside the ticket gates (open 11:00 am to 7:00 pm at the time of writing).

Which platform is which

Tokyo Station has above-ground platforms numbered 1 to 23, plus two separate sets of underground platforms. The two underground sets are the ones that catch people out.

Notice that the shinkansen is split into two groups: platforms 14–19 for trains heading west (Kyoto and Osaka), and 20–23 for trains heading north and to Kanazawa. Check your ticket for the line name before you head for the gates. New to the bullet train? See how to ride the Shinkansen.

Real transfer times from the shinkansen

JR East publishes standard transfer times from the shinkansen to other lines at Tokyo Station. These include walking through the station, so use them when you plan connections:

From the shinkansen to…Standard time
The other shinkansen group (14–19 ↔ 20–23)8 min
Yamanote, Keihin-Tohoku, Tokaido, Tohoku lines10 min
Chuo Line, Sobu Line, Yokosuka Line (incl. Narita Airport trains)15 min
Keiyo Line (for Maihama / Tokyo Disney Resort)20 min

JR East notes that these times can change during station improvement work. With big suitcases or children, add a few minutes. The Keiyo Line platforms are a long walk underground, so don't book a tight connection to them.

Buying and collecting tickets

  • According to Tokyo Station City, the ticket offices inside the gates sell tickets for today only. To buy for another day, use an office outside the gates (for example at the Yaesu North Gate or the Marunouchi underground gates).
  • Tickets booked on JR East's Eki-net can be collected at JR East reserved-seat ticket machines and ticket offices, and at JR Central ticket machines (some excluded). JR East says they can't be collected at JR Central's staffed counters.
  • Need an IC card? See where to get a Welcome Suica, or our overview of Suica, PASMO and ICOCA.

Where to leave your luggage

Coin lockers fill up fast at busy times. These are the official options listed by Tokyo Station City:

  • Suica lockers: the TOKYO STATION NAVI app shows which lockers have space in real time, so you can check before you walk across the station.
  • Shinkansen Parcel Service & Baggage Storage: outside the Yaesu South Gate, open 5:30 to 23:00.
  • JR EAST Travel Service Center Baggage Storage: at the Marunouchi North Gate, outside the ticket gates.
  • Cloak Service and BAGGAGE STORAGE +: on B1 inside GRANSTA TOKYO.
  • Hakobyun Quick: a same-train luggage delivery service on JR East shinkansen routes. Requests can be made until 30 minutes before the train leaves.

If you'd rather not carry big bags through the station at all, luggage forwarding to your next hotel is often the easiest choice.

Tips from the railway side

  • Pick a gate, not "Tokyo Station." Meeting someone at "Tokyo Station" can mean a 10-minute walk in the wrong direction. Agree on a gate name, such as Marunouchi North Gate.
  • Follow the line colors and names on the signs, not the platform number alone. The underground Sobu and Keiyo platforms reuse the numbers 1–4.
  • Ask at a staffed window if your ticket won't open a gate. Staff can fix it quickly, and it's much faster than trying other gates.
