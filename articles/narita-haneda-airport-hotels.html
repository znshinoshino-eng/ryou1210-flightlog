Narita and Haneda Airport Hotels: When Staying at the Airport Is Worth It

Airport guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: an airport hotel is worth it when your flight lands after the last train or leaves before the first one, and it matters most at Narita, which is far from central Tokyo. Haneda has hotels inside or directly connected to all three terminals. Narita has a capsule hotel next to Terminal 2 and a group of nearby hotels you reach by shuttle bus. For a normal daytime flight, staying in the city is usually the better choice.

When an airport hotel makes sense

Trains and buses into Tokyo stop running around midnight and start again early in the morning. That leaves a gap. If your flight falls in or near that gap, an airport hotel saves you a late taxi ride or a rushed pre-dawn trip across the city.

  • You land late at night. Immigration, baggage claim and customs take time. If you'll come out after the last train or bus, a bed a few minutes' walk away is often simpler than a long taxi ride. Our guide to arriving late at Narita or Haneda covers last trains and taxi fares.
  • Your flight leaves very early. Getting to the airport before the first trains arrive means a taxi or a very short night. Sleeping at the airport lets you walk to check-in.
  • You're flying out of Narita in the morning. Narita is a long way from central Tokyo. Even when trains are running, a morning flight from Narita means an early start from a city hotel, especially with big suitcases.
  • You're connecting between international flights at Haneda. Haneda has a transit hotel inside the international departure area, for passengers who don't enter Japan (more below).

When it's not worth it: if you land in the afternoon or leave in the late morning, you'll usually be better off in the city, where you're close to sightseeing. See where to stay in Tokyo on a first trip for which station area suits you.

Haneda: hotels inside the terminals

Haneda Airport's official site lists hotels in each of its three terminals. This matters because Haneda's terminals don't all stay open overnight. According to the airport, the domestic areas of Terminals 1 and 2 are generally open from 5:00 to 24:00, and after closing it is generally not possible to stay in the lobby. Terminal 3 is generally open 24 hours, but only people who have used or are planning to use international flights can stay inside overnight.

A few details from the hotels' own websites that are useful to know:

  • First Cabin Haneda Terminal 1 is a "cabin" hotel: compact single rooms separated by curtains rather than lockable doors, with shared baths and showers and separate men's and women's areas. Because the terminal closes overnight, late arrivals use a night entrance near bus stop 8 and call staff to be let in. Short daytime stays are also offered on a walk-in basis, subject to availability. Note that luggage can only be held after check-in and on the day of your stay.
  • Haneda Excel Hotel Tokyu is about a 3-minute walk from the north exit of the Tokyo Monorail's Haneda Airport Terminal 2 Station, and about 5 minutes from the Terminal 2 exit of Keikyu's Haneda Airport Terminal 1/2 Station. The lobby entrance closes after midnight, so late guests use the night elevator on 1F.
  • The Royal Park Hotel Tokyo Haneda has its entrance in the 3F departure lobby of Terminal 3, past airline check-in counter N. It's about a 3-minute walk from the Tokyo Monorail's Terminal 3 station and about 5 minutes from Keikyu's Terminal 3 station.
  • Villa Fontaine Grand Haneda Airport describes itself as directly connected to Terminal 3. You can check rooms and rates for your dates at Villa Fontaine Grand Haneda Airport on Klook (affiliate link).

If your hotel is in a different terminal from your flight, Haneda runs a free shuttle bus between terminals. The Royal Park Hotel says the ride from Terminal 1 or 2 to Terminal 3 takes about 10 to 15 minutes.

Haneda's transit hotel: only if you don't enter Japan

The transit part of The Royal Park Hotel Tokyo Haneda is inside the restricted international departure area of Terminal 3. The hotel says it is only for passengers who don't intend to enter Japan (for example, connecting from one international flight to another), or for people who have already completed departure procedures. You need a boarding pass for an international flight.

The important point for first-time visitors: if you arrive at Haneda on an international flight and enter Japan, you can't use the transit hotel. Book one of the landside hotels instead. The hotel also says check-in is not available between 1:00 and 5:00 a.m.

Narita: the capsule hotel and hotel shuttle buses

Narita is set up differently. According to Narita Airport, there is one hotel at the terminals themselves: nine hours Narita Airport, a capsule hotel next to Terminal 2, before the security check. The airport lists it on Terminal 2's B1F and also describes it as being in the P2 car park building connected to Terminal 2. Its front desk is staffed 24 hours. The airport recommends booking ahead because it can be full on the day.

The airport lists these prices for daytime use (tax included, at the time of writing in October 2026):

  • Day use (nap): ¥1,500 for the first hour, then ¥500 per extra hour
  • Shower only: ¥1,000 (up to 1 hour)

For overnight rates, contact the hotel directly, as the airport doesn't list them.

For a regular hotel room, you'll be staying a short bus ride away. Narita Airport's site lists hotels near the airport, and hotels around Narita Station, that run shuttle buses, with the bus stop numbers at Terminal 1 and Terminal 2. Travel times the airport gives range from about 5 minutes for the closest hotels to about 30 to 35 minutes for the farthest. Hotels listed include Hotel Nikko Narita, Narita Tobu Hotel Airport, Hilton Narita, ANA Crowne Plaza Narita and others.

Two things to check with your hotel before you go:

  • Fares and times. The airport's page doesn't say whether the shuttles are free or how often they run, so check the hotel's own website.
  • Whether the bus is still running. The airport notes that buses to some hotels are subject to change or cancellation, and asks you to confirm with your hotel in advance. This is especially important if you land late.

If you'd rather wait in the terminal at Narita

Narita's terminals don't fully close at night, but only some floors stay open. According to the airport, in Terminal 1 the 1F (international arrivals) and B1F are open 24 hours; in Terminal 2 the 2F domestic arrivals area, 1F and B1F are open 24 hours; and in Terminal 3 the 2F and 1F are open 24 hours. Other floors, including the international departure floors in Terminals 1 and 2, open from 5:00 to 24:00.

Terminal 2 also has a café and refreshment space you can rent by the hour without a reservation. The airport warns that there may be cleaning or construction work near waiting areas early in the morning, so don't count on a quiet night.

Before a long wait, make sure your phone will last: see our eSIM, pocket Wi-Fi and power bank guide.

Railway worker's tips for airport nights

  • Check your terminal first. At Haneda, a hotel in the wrong terminal means a shuttle bus ride. At Narita, check which terminal your airline uses and which bus stop your hotel's shuttle uses.
  • Send big luggage ahead. If you're staying in a capsule or cabin hotel, there's limited room for suitcases. You can send bags between the city and the airport instead: see our luggage forwarding guide.
  • Plan the morning trip the night before. If you stay in the city instead, work out your first train to the airport in advance. Our guides to Narita to Tokyo and Haneda to Tokyo explain the train and bus options.
  • For families and early starts, a pre-booked car is an alternative. A driver picks you up at your city hotel at the time you choose.
