Kansai Airport to Osaka and Kyoto: Haruka, Rapi:t or Limousine Bus?

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: pick by where you're sleeping. Staying around Namba? Take the Nankai rapi:t (34 minutes at the fastest). Heading to Shin-Osaka or Kyoto? Take the JR Haruka (about 50 and 75 minutes). Staying at a hotel near Osaka Station (Umeda), Shinsaibashi or Universal Studios Japan with big suitcases? The limousine bus drops you closer to the door, with no stairs or transfers.

Kansai International Airport (KIX) sits on an island in Osaka Bay, so every option starts with a ride across the bridge. Below are the three main choices, with journey times taken from Kansai Airport's own access page and from the operators themselves.

Nankai rapi:t: the quick way to Namba

The rapi:t is Nankai Electric Railway's airport limited express. It runs between Kansai-Airport Station and Nankai Namba Station, in the south of central Osaka, close to Dotonbori and Shinsaibashi. Nankai gives the fastest run as 34 minutes. Kansai Airport's access page lists about 34 minutes for the rapi:t α and about 39 minutes for the rapi:t β, which makes a few more stops.

Good to know before you ride:

  • All seats are reserved. You need a limited express ticket on top of a normal train ticket. Nankai's ticket counters at the airport have separate windows for people exchanging an online or travel-agency booking and for people buying on the spot.
  • Two classes. Regular Seats (cars 1 to 4, four seats per row) and roomier Super Seats (cars 5 and 6, three seats per row).
  • No-reservation alternative. Nankai's ordinary Airport Express also runs to Namba. Kansai Airport lists it at about 43 minutes, so it's only a little slower.

JR Haruka: best for Shin-Osaka and Kyoto

The Haruka is JR West's airport limited express. It's the one to take for Kyoto, and for Shin-Osaka if you're catching a shinkansen straight away. Kansai Airport's access page gives these approximate times for the Haruka:

  • Tennoji: about 30 minutes
  • Shin-Osaka: about 50 minutes
  • Kyoto: about 75 minutes

If you'd rather not pay the limited express charge, JR's Kansai Airport Rapid Service is the slower, no-frills alternative. The airport lists it at about 45 minutes to Tennoji and about 65 minutes to Osaka Station. It's an ordinary commuter train, so expect luggage to be a squeeze at busy times.

The JR Kansai Area Pass covers the Haruka. If you're using that pass anyway, the airport ride is a good way to get value from it. Limousine bus: door-to-door-ish with luggage

Kansai Airport Transportation Enterprise runs the airport limousine buses from both Terminal 1 and Terminal 2. You put your suitcase in the hold, sit down, and get off at a big station or right outside certain hotels. Here's what the operator's timetable pages show at the time of writing (October 2026):

  • Osaka Station (Umeda): about 60 minutes from Terminal 1. Stops include Herbis Osaka, Hankyu Sanban Gai, Candeo Hotels Osaka The Tower and Hotel Hankyu Respire Osaka. ¥1,800 adult, ¥900 child, one way.
  • Shinsaibashi / Kintetsu Uehommachi: about 55 minutes from Terminal 1 to Kintetsu Uehommachi, with a Shinsaibashi stop at Hotel Nikko Osaka. ¥1,800 adult, ¥900 child, one way.
  • Universal Studios Japan route: about 1 hour 10 minutes from Terminal 1 to USJ, also serving Tempozan (Kaiyukan), Hotel Kintetsu Universal City and other bay-area hotels. ¥1,800 adult, ¥900 child, one way. This route runs only during the day, with the last departure from Terminal 1 at 17:10 on the current timetable.
  • Kyoto Station (Hachijo-guchi): about 1 hour 25 to 1 hour 30 minutes from Terminal 1. ¥2,800 adult, ¥1,400 child, one way.

Round-trip tickets valid for 14 days are also sold on these routes (for example ¥3,300 adult for Osaka Station, Shinsaibashi or USJ, and ¥5,100 for Kyoto). Road traffic can make the bus slower than the times above, so leave a buffer on the way back to the airport.

Landing late? What to check

If your flight lands late in the evening, immigration and baggage claim can take a while. A few practical points:

  • Look up the last train for your exact date on the JR West or Nankai website before you fly, and compare it with your scheduled arrival plus at least an hour. Don't rely on a last-train time you read in a blog (including this one), because timetables change.
  • The Kyoto bus runs late. On the current limousine bus timetable, the last departure to Kyoto Station leaves Terminal 1 at 23:20 (23:07 from Terminal 2). Check the operator's timetable for your date.
  • If you're arriving at Terminal 2, remember the train station is next to Terminal 1. Buses also stop at Terminal 2, which can be easier at night.
  • Have a backup plan. If your flight is badly delayed, an airport-area hotel for one night is often less stressful than racing for the last connection.

The flight home: send your bags ahead

On your last day, the same options work in reverse. But dragging suitcases through Kyoto or Namba on a sightseeing day is no fun. A luggage delivery service picks your bags up at your Osaka or Kyoto hotel and has them waiting at KIX, so you can travel to the airport light. Which one should you book?

Match the train to your hotel, not the other way round. Namba means rapi:t. Kyoto or Shin-Osaka means Haruka. A hotel that's a stop on the limousine bus route, or a family with big bags heading for USJ, means the bus. Any of them gets you there in about an hour or so.
