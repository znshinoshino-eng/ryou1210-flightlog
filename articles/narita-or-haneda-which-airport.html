Narita or Haneda? Which Tokyo Airport to Fly Into

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: if the flights cost about the same, Haneda is usually the easier choice for a first trip. It sits inside Tokyo, and the train into the city takes 11–13 minutes to the first big interchange for ¥327–¥519 on an IC card. Narita is about 60 km away in Chiba Prefecture. The fastest trains still get you to Ueno or Tokyo Station in under an hour, but they cost ¥2,580–¥3,140. Before you choose, compare the full trip: the fare difference, your arrival time and where you are staying.

Below I compare the two airports the way I would for a friend: distance, the main trains, the fares and times the operators publish, and what happens if you land late at night. All prices are adult one-way fares at the time of writing (November 2026).

Where the two airports are

Japan's national tourism organization (JNTO) describes Narita as "about 60 kilometers east of central Tokyo in Chiba Prefecture". It has three terminals. The airport says "Different terminals are used depending on flights. Check your terminal by flight search", so look up your flight on the Narita website before you travel.

Haneda is inside Tokyo itself. JNTO says it is "around 30 minutes by train from the center of Tokyo" and calls it "more convenient to the city" than Narita. International flights use Terminal 3 and the international area of Terminal 2. Our Haneda terminals guide explains which one you need.

A few notes on the table:

  • Skyliner. Keisei's FAQ says "A one-way fare on the Skyliner costs 2,580 yen". That is the basic fare plus a Skyliner ticket for a reserved seat. Keisei has also applied for government approval to raise the Skyliner charge by ¥200 for adults. It plans to do this on 13 March 2027, so check again if you travel after that date.
  • Narita Express. JR East lists ¥3,140 to Tokyo in the Ordinary Car and says the price is the "Same price throughout the year". Narita Airport lists the N'EX for "Access to Tokyo, Shibuya, Shinjuku and West Tokyo Area".
  • Low-cost bus. Narita Airport lists ¥1,500 to Tokyo Station, Ginza Station and Shinonome, with the note "Except for late night and early morning flights". Check the fare for your time if you land very late or leave very early.
  • Haneda. The Monorail says it runs "on an average of every five minutes" to Hamamatsucho. Keikyu's fares above are IC card fares. An IC card such as Suica or PASMO makes this the simplest arrival in Tokyo.

What the difference costs a couple or a family

For one person, taking the Skyliner or N'EX from Narita costs roughly ¥2,000–¥2,800 more each way than the train from Haneda, and takes around 30–40 minutes longer. The slower Keisei Access Express (¥1,190 to Oshiage) narrows the fare gap to under ¥900, but takes longer. For a family of four that adds up quickly over a round trip. Narita can still work out cheaper overall if your flights there cost much less. So compare the ticket prices for both airports, then add the ground transport.

Narita has one discount for visitors. JR East's N'EX TOKYO Round Trip Ticket costs ¥5,200 for adults (¥2,600 for children). It covers one N'EX ride each way within 14 days. JR East says "This offer is for holders of non-Japanese passports". You show your passport when you pick it up at Narita Airport's JR stations. That works out at ¥2,600 per ride.

Which airport suits where you are staying

  • Ueno, Asakusa, Nippori or Oshiage: Narita works well here. The Skyliner goes straight to Nippori and Ueno, and the Access Express runs to Oshiage. From Haneda, Keikyu lists Asakusa at 32 minutes.
  • Shinagawa, Ginza, Shimbashi, Hamamatsucho or Yokohama: Haneda is the clear winner. Keikyu lists Yokohama at 23 minutes for ¥363 on an IC card.
  • Shinjuku or Shibuya: both work. From Haneda you change trains after the short ride into the city. From Narita, the N'EX serves both areas, though the trip is longer.

If you are carrying a lot of luggage or travelling with small children, a private car transfer takes the stress out of either airport.

Arriving late at night or leaving early

This is where the two airports differ most.

Haneda. The airport's FAQ says Terminal 3 is "generally open 24 hours a day", though hours "may change depending on the situation". It adds that "only those who have used or are planning to use international flights can stay inside the terminal overnight". JNTO says the earliest train "arrives a little after 5 a.m." and the last train "departs around midnight". If you land close to midnight, you may still catch a train.

Narita. The airport says "Available hours will vary according to location", and some floors are open 24 hours, including the Terminal 1 international arrivals floor. It asks travellers to "check the available hours of the terminals" before using them at night or early in the morning. A late arrival at Narita can leave you with a long, tiring trip into Tokyo. Our guide to arriving late at Narita or Haneda covers your options, and airport hotels are worth a look for very late or very early flights.

Flying home from Narita? The airport advises you to "Arrive at the airport at least two hours before your departure time". Allow for the longer train ride when you plan your last morning.

My simple rule

  • Choose Haneda if the fares are similar, if you land late, or if you are staying on the south or west side of central Tokyo.
  • Narita is fine if it saves you real money on flights, if you are staying near Ueno or Asakusa, or if the N'EX round-trip ticket suits your trip.
  • Either way, set up an IC card and get mobile data working before you land, so you can check platforms and connections on the train.
