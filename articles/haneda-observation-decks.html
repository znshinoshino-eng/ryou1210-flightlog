Plane Spotting at Haneda Airport: A Complete Guide for International Visitors

Plane spotting · Airport observation decks

One of the best things about Haneda Airport (HND) is that you don't need a boarding pass to watch planes up close. All three terminals have free public observation decks, open to anyone. But they're not interchangeable — each one faces a different direction, has a different atmosphere, and suits a different kind of shot. This guide breaks down the three decks so you can pick the right one before you make the trip out.

Terminal 1 Observation Deck: Mt. Fuji and golden-hour shots

The Terminal 1 deck faces west. On a clear day, you can frame departing or taxiing aircraft against Mt. Fuji or the Yokohama Landmark Tower skyline in the distance. It's built as a wooden deck with benches, so it's comfortable to just sit and watch planes for a while rather than only shoot. Because it faces west, this is the deck most spotters head to for sunset shots — the warm, low light paired with an aircraft silhouette is a classic Haneda shot.

Terminal 2 Observation Deck: Tokyo Bay views and night photography

The Terminal 2 deck looks out over Tokyo Bay, which makes it a strong choice if you want water in your background rather than city skyline. On the south side of the deck there's an area called the "Starry Stage," lit by roughly 4,000 embedded LEDs — at night, combined with the runway lights in the distance, it creates a genuinely photogenic scene. It's popular with photographers and couples alike after dark.

Terminal 3 Observation Deck: open 24 hours, best for international carriers

Terminal 3 is Haneda's international terminal, and its deck is the only one of the three that's open 24 hours a day. If you're chasing an early-morning or late-night arrival, or you only have a short window on a weekday morning, this is often your only option. It's also the best deck if you specifically want to photograph international airlines rather than domestic Japanese carriers — you'll see a much wider mix of liveries here.

Moving between terminals

All three terminals are connected by a free shuttle bus, and the ride between them takes roughly 5–10 minutes. That means it's realistic to check more than one deck in a single visit — a useful option if you want to match the day's wind direction (which determines which runway is active) with the best-facing deck.
