Japan's New Fees and Rules for Visitors in 2026–27

Travel prep · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: a trip to Japan now costs a little more in fixed fees, and a few things work differently. The departure tax went up to ¥3,000 in July 2026. Kyoto's lodging tax went up in March 2026. The JR Pass now has two prices depending on where you buy it. And from 1 November 2026, tax-free shopping works as a refund. JESTA, the planned online travel authorization, is not running yet.

The departure tax is now ¥3,000

Japan's International Tourist Tax, often called the departure tax, is ¥3,000 per departure from Japan on or after 1 July 2026. Before that it was ¥1,000. There is one transition rule: according to the Japan Tourism Agency, the "previous tax rate (JPY 1,000) applies if you depart using eligible tickets issued on or before June 30, 2026."

You normally don't pay it separately. "As a general rule, air and sea carriers collect the International Tourist Tax from passengers departing Japan," usually by adding it to the ticket price. So if you see a ¥3,000 line in your fare breakdown, that's it.

Two exemptions matter to most travelers. Children under 2 don't pay it, and neither do transit passengers who leave Japan within 24 hours of arriving.

Kyoto's lodging tax went up in March 2026

Kyoto City charges an accommodation tax per person, per night, and the amount depends on the room charge (excluding meals and consumption tax). These rates apply from 1 March 2026:

Kyoto City asks guests to pay it to the hotel or inn where they stay. If you booked through a booking site or travel agent, the city says the tax may already be included in what you paid them. In the ¥6,000 to under ¥20,000 band, that's ¥400 per person per night, so it's worth checking whether your booking already includes it.

The JR Pass has two prices

For purchases made on or after 1 October 2026, the JR Group raised the Japan Rail Pass price at overseas agencies and JR sales offices. But "prices via the online purchasing service will remain unchanged for a limited time." JR says it will announce the end of this price freeze "as soon as it is decided". No end date had been announced at the time of writing.

Tax-free shopping is now a refund

JNTO puts it simply: "Beginning November 1, 2026, travelers will pay the full tax-inclusive price when purchasing items at tax-free shops." The tax comes back after Customs confirms at departure that you're taking the goods out of Japan.

  • Minimum spend: "Purchases totaling 5,000 yen or more (tax excluded) per store per day are eligible."
  • Deadline: "Within 90 days from the date of purchase, you are required to present your passport at a tax-free procedure terminal."
  • No customs check, no refund: the National Tax Agency warns, "If you do not complete customs confirmation, you cannot receive a consumption tax refund." It also says "Customs confirmation cannot be completed after you have checked your hand luggage".
  • Who pays it back: "The refund will be provided by each tax-free shop or by a refund service provider entrusted by the shop."
  • Packaging: "Special packaging for consumables will be abolished." Items consumed in Japan are not eligible.

One more trap from JNTO: if even one item on a receipt is missing at the check, "none of the items associated with that receipt" qualify. Our tax-free refund guide walks through the shop, the kiosk and Visit Japan Web step by step.

You can tap a credit card at some ticket gates

Some railways now accept contactless credit cards right at the ticket gate. For example, Osaka Metro accepts "Visa, Mastercard®, JCB, American Express, Diners Club, Discover, UnionPay" on the Subway and New Tram, and says "No prior registration is required". Tokyo Metro says "Tap to Ride Service is available on all Tokyo Metro lines," except for passengers boarding at Nakano Station.

Three rules to know:

  • On both subways it's adult fare only. Osaka Metro: "even if a child uses the service, the adult fare will be charged."
  • On Tokyo Metro, only the cardholder can use the card: "Use by anyone other than the person named on the card is not permitted."
  • It won't carry you from Tokyo Metro onto JR East. Tokyo Metro: "Passengers cannot travel beyond Tokyo Metro onto JR EAST using the Tap to Ride Service."

Rules differ by operator, so check before you rely on it. See which trains accept contactless cards and our guide to Suica, PASMO and ICOCA.

JESTA: coming, but not yet

JESTA (Japan Electronic System for Travel Authorization) is Japan's planned online travel authorization. The law creating it was promulgated on 5 June 2026. It will apply to visa-exempt visitors coming for short stays such as tourism.

It is not in operation yet, and there is nothing to apply for. The start date will be set by cabinet order, no later than 31 March 2029, and the government says it aims to introduce JESTA in fiscal year 2028. A fee is planned, but the amount has not been set. Be careful with any website that offers a "JESTA application" today.

Also worth knowing: Mt. Fuji climbing fees

If you plan to climb Mt. Fuji in summer, both prefectures charged ¥4,000 per person per climb in the 2026 season (Shizuoka calls it an entry fee). On the Yamanashi side (Yoshida Trail) the rules applied from 1 July to 10 September, with a cap of 4,000 climbers a day. On the Shizuoka side, pre-registration was required. The 2026 season is over, and Shizuoka says 2027 details will be announced once decided. Not climbing? Our Mt. Fuji and Hakone day trips guide covers the easier options.

A quick checklist before you fly

  • Look for the ¥3,000 departure tax in your fare breakdown. As a general rule, the airline collects it with your ticket.
  • Staying in Kyoto? See if your booking includes the lodging tax. If not, you pay it at the hotel or inn.
  • Buying a JR Pass? Compare the official online price with what an agency charges.
  • Shopping tax-free? Keep your passport and the goods handy for the customs check at departure, within 90 days of purchase.
  • JESTA isn't running yet, so there's nothing to apply for.
