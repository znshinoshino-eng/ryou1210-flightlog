Where to Stay in Tokyo on a First Trip: Shinjuku, Shibuya, Ueno, Tokyo Station or Asakusa?

Area guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: pick your area by your airport and your next train out of Tokyo, not by the neighborhood's reputation. Flying into Narita and heading to Kyoto by shinkansen? Tokyo Station or Ueno make life easy. Flying into Haneda? Areas on the Keikyu and Toei Asakusa lines, like Asakusa, get you there without a change. Want nightlife and day trips to Hakone or Mt. Fuji? Shinjuku. All five areas below are good choices; they just suit different trips.

I work on Japan's railways, and the most common hotel mistake I see is not the area itself. It's a hotel that's a 15-minute walk from the "right" station, dragging a suitcase. Wherever you stay, check which station exit the hotel is near, and how many minutes it is on foot.

The five areas at a glance

Tokyo Station: best for shinkansen trips

Every shinkansen line serving Tokyo starts here: the Tokaido and Sanyo Shinkansen to Kyoto, Osaka and Hiroshima, and the Tohoku, Hokkaido, Yamagata, Akita, Joetsu and Hokuriku Shinkansen to the north and to Kanazawa. If your trip has a day where you check out and take the bullet train, being able to walk to the platform is a real advantage.

  • Narita: the Narita Express runs direct. JR East says the fastest train takes 53 minutes.
  • Haneda: about 30 minutes, by the airport's estimate.
  • Around town: the Yamanote, Chuo and Keihin-Tohoku lines, and the Narita Express from the underground Sobu platforms.

The catch: the station is huge, and it has two sides, Marunouchi and Yaesu. A hotel "at Tokyo Station" on the wrong side can mean a long walk with bags. Our Tokyo Station guide explains the layout, and JR East's own transfer times are a good reminder of the walking distances.

Ueno: best for Narita and the north

Ueno is where the Keisei Skyliner ends. All seats are reserved, and Keisei gives 41 minutes from Ueno to Narita Airport Terminal 2·3. The Skyliner also stops at Nippori, one station before Ueno, where you can change to JR lines. Its final stop is Keisei-Ueno Station, so when you compare hotels, check the walk to Keisei-Ueno, not just to "Ueno".

  • Shinkansen: JR Ueno is a stop on the Tohoku, Hokkaido, Yamagata, Akita, Joetsu and Hokuriku Shinkansen, so trips to Kanazawa, Nagano or Sendai can start here. Trains to Kyoto and Osaka don't stop at Ueno; for those, ride the Yamanote or Keihin-Tohoku Line to Tokyo Station first.
  • Around town: the Yamanote and Keihin-Tohoku lines, plus the Ginza and Hibiya subway lines. The Ginza Line runs straight to Asakusa, Ginza and Shibuya.

Ueno Park and its museums are right by the station. It's a practical, good-value base, especially if Narita is your airport.

Asakusa: best for reaching both airports without a change

Asakusa's trick is the Toei Asakusa Line. Its trains run through onto the Keikyu Line to Haneda and onto the Keisei Line to Narita, so you can reach either airport without changing trains. Toei itself says you can go directly from Narita to Asakusa without a transfer. Keisei gives about 50 minutes from Narita Terminal 2·3 to Asakusa, and the Access Express fare at the time of writing was ¥1,290, or ¥1,276 with an IC card. From Haneda, the airport estimates about 40 minutes.

  • Day trips: Tobu's SPACIA X limited express leaves from Asakusa for Nikko and Kinugawa-Onsen. On the timetable we checked, Asakusa to Tobu-Nikko took about 1 hour 50 minutes.
  • Around town: the Ginza Line (Ueno, Ginza, Shibuya) and the Toei Asakusa Line (Nihombashi, Higashi-ginza).

The downside is that Asakusa isn't on the Yamanote Line or the shinkansen, so trips west of the city take a little longer. Not every Asakusa Line train goes to the airport either; check that yours shows Haneda or Narita as its destination.

Shibuya: best for nightlife and the west side

Shibuya has JR's Yamanote, Saikyo and Shonan-Shinjuku lines, and three Tokyo Metro lines: Ginza, Hanzomon and Fukutoshin. The Narita Express stops here, which makes it one of the few nightlife areas with a direct train from Narita.

  • Haneda: about 45 minutes, by the airport's estimate.
  • Shinkansen: none here. Allow time to get to Tokyo Station (or Shinagawa) first.

The walks between companies' lines at Shibuya can be long. If you have big suitcases, look for a hotel close to the exit you'll actually use.

Shinjuku: best for Hakone, Fuji and buses

Shinjuku is several stations in one, run by JR East, Odakyu, Keio, Toei and Tokyo Metro. The Narita Express stops here. Odakyu's Romancecar leaves for Hakone (about 80 minutes to Hakone-Yumoto, all seats reserved), and the Busta Shinjuku terminal at the station's south side is Shinjuku's highway bus terminal. If Hakone is on your list, the Hakone Freepass on Klook (affiliate link) includes a round trip on the Odakyu Line between Shinjuku and Odawara plus unlimited rides on Hakone's trains, cable car, ropeway, cruise boats and designated buses. The Romancecar needs a separate limited express ticket.

  • Airport buses: Airport Limousine buses to both Narita and Haneda stop at Shinjuku Station, the Shinjuku Expressway Bus Terminal and several big West Shinjuku hotels, which is very handy with luggage.
  • Haneda by train: about 50 minutes, by the airport's estimate.

The station is big and confusing at first. Our Shinjuku Station guide shows which gate to use for each side.

Tips from the railway side

  • Count the walk, not the distance on the map. A hotel 5 minutes from a small exit can be better than one "at" a giant station.
  • Send big bags ahead. If you're moving hotels or going on to Kyoto, luggage forwarding saves you carrying suitcases through rush hour.
  • Get an IC card or a subway pass. Wherever you stay, you'll use several companies' trains. An IC card covers them all. If you'll ride the subway a lot, the Tokyo Subway Ticket covers all Tokyo Metro and Toei lines for 24, 48 or 72 hours (¥1,000 / ¥1,500 / ¥2,000 for adults at the official price at the time of writing). It's also sold as the Tokyo Subway Ticket on Klook (affiliate link). It doesn't cover JR lines such as the Yamanote Line.
  • Arriving late? Check the last trains before you choose. Our late-arrival guide covers the options, and our Narita and Haneda guides cover tickets into the city.
