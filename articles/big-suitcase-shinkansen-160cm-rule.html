Big Suitcase on the Shinkansen: The 160 cm Rule, Oversized-Baggage Seats, and When to Send It Ahead

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: if your suitcase's length + width + height add up to more than 160 cm, you need a reserved seat with an oversized baggage area on the Tokaido, Sanyo, Kyushu and Nishi-Kyushu shinkansen. The seat costs the same as an ordinary reserved seat — the reservation is free — but board without one and staff charge you ¥1,000. Under 160 cm, nothing changes. Over 250 cm, the bag can't ride at all. If the right seats are sold out, send the bag ahead instead: takkyubin takes a day or two, and JR West's same-day delivery moves bags between Kyoto/Osaka and Hiroshima or Fukuoka on the day itself.

Measure your bag first: the 160 cm test

JR's rule is one measurement. Add the three sides of your bag together — length + width + height, wheels and handles included — and compare with 160 cm (about 63 inches). A standard large checked suitcase often lands right around this line, so measure yours rather than guessing from the size in inches.

Your bag (length + width + height)What it meansWhat to do
160 cm or lessNormal baggageNo reservation needed — overhead rack, footwell, or deck baggage area.
161 to 250 cmOversized baggageReserve an oversized-baggage seat (see below).
Over 250 cmToo big to carry onCan't be brought on the train. Send it ahead.

Two more limits apply to everything you carry on, whatever its size: at most two pieces per person, each 30 kg or less and no longer than 200 cm on its longest side.

Which shinkansen lines the rule applies to

The rule covers the JR Central, JR West and JR Kyushu lines — one continuous route from Tokyo to Kagoshima-Chuo, plus the Nishi-Kyushu line to Nagasaki. That is the route most first-time visitors ride.

LineOversized-baggage seat needed?
Tokaido shinkansen (Tokyo – Shin-Osaka)Yes
Sanyo shinkansen (Shin-Osaka – Hakata)Yes
Kyushu shinkansen (Hakata – Kagoshima-Chuo)Yes

Tohoku, Hokuriku, Joetsu, Hokkaido, Akita and Yamagata shinkansenNo
Ordinary (non-shinkansen) trainsNo

On the lines without the rule, the 250 cm ceiling still applies, and you use whatever space is free — overhead racks, the footwell, or the luggage area — on a first-come, first-served basis. Heading north to Kanazawa or Sendai, the seat type doesn't matter — just board early.

How to book a seat with an oversized baggage area

These seats are the last row of certain cars, with the bag going in the space between the seatbacks and the end wall. In an ordinary car the space is shared by the five passengers who booked that row, so keep your bag within your share. The same seats exist in Green Cars.

Book the seat when you buy your ticket: on SmartEX, JR West's online reservation, or JR Kyushu's reservation service, at a reserved-seat ticket machine, or at a ticket office — just ask for a seat with an oversized baggage area. Japan Rail Pass holders do the same at a ticket office, or through the JAPAN RAIL PASS Reservation site. It costs exactly the same as an ordinary reserved seat; there is no surcharge. See our guide to buying shinkansen tickets before you arrive for which booking site serves which line.

Book early: there aren't many of these seats. On a 16-car Tokaido train, JR Central counts 57 on a Nozomi, 42 on a Hikari and only 17 on a Kodama. On busy days they go first, and every train you take needs its own oversized-baggage seat — you can't just switch to the next departure and keep your bag.

  • Strollers, musical instruments and sports equipment are exempt from the rule, even over 160 cm — but reserve the seat if you want to use the oversized baggage area for them.
  • Anyone can reserve one for extra space, for example with a stroller. JR asks you to leave them for people who need them when you don't.
  • Some decks have lockable baggage compartments (up to 80 × 60 × 40 cm), currently free without a reservation on a trial basis. They fit a cabin-size case, not an oversized one.

What happens if you board without the right seat

Two things, and neither is a loophole. First, an oversized bag is not allowed in a non-reserved car at all — so the "just hop on the next train" option closes the moment your bag crosses 160 cm. Second, if you board a reserved car without the oversized-baggage seat, staff charge a ¥1,000 carry-on oversized baggage fee (including tax) and decide where your bag goes. Treat that as a penalty for boarding wrong, not a paid upgrade you can rely on: the space may already be taken, and on a full train there may be nowhere good to put the bag. When forwarding the bag is easier

Sometimes the honest answer is not to carry the bag at all — Japan's luggage forwarding is built for exactly this.

Takkyubin is the everyday choice: hand the bag to your hotel front desk, a convenience store or an airport counter, and it turns up at your next hotel a day or two later. Our luggage forwarding guide has the full how-to, prices by size, and the airport cut-offs. The catch is the wait — send it the day before you travel, and carry an overnight bag on the train.

Same-day Delivery WEST is the fast option on one corridor: JR West carries your bag on the shinkansen from Kyoto, Osaka or Shin-Osaka to Hiroshima or Hakata, or the other way round. Drop it off by 14:00 at Shin-Osaka, 13:00 at Kyoto or Hiroshima, 12:30 at Hakata, or noon at Osaka, and collect it at the destination station by 19:00 or at your hotel by 21:00. Bags must be within 200 cm in total and under 30 kg, and hotel delivery needs a real room booking — unstaffed rentals don't qualify. Book through an online travel agency and register the address on the carrier's site up to a week ahead; cancelling after 23:59 the day before costs the full price. At ¥11,000–¥12,000 a bag it's dear — two bags to Fukuoka cost ¥24,000 — so weigh it against a free reserved seat first.

On the Tokyo–Kyoto corridor, JR Central points to a similar same-day service, LUGGAGE EXPRESS, between Kyoto and Tokyo stations or hotels — check its site for routes and prices.

My rule of thumb: one bag you can lift — carry it; the seat costs nothing extra. Two big bags, small children, or a sightseeing stop — forward them and ride with a day bag.

Quick checklist before you travel

  • Measure the bag — length + width + height, wheels and handles included. Over 160 cm means a reserved oversized-baggage seat on the Tokaido, Sanyo, Kyushu and Nishi-Kyushu shinkansen.
  • Book the seat with the ticket — SmartEX, e5489, JR Kyushu's site, a machine, or a ticket office. JR Pass holders, say so at the office.
  • Keep the bag under 250 cm in total and 30 kg, and take at most two pieces per person.
  • If the seats are sold out, forward the bag — takkyubin the day before, or same-day delivery on the Kyoto/Osaka–Hiroshima/Fukuoka corridor.
  • Don't count on the ¥1,000 fee as a plan. It's what happens when you board wrong, and the space may not be there.
