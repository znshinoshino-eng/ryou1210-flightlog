Japan Rail Passes: Which Ones Are Actually Worth It

Rail guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Prices checked on Klook in October 2026

Short answer: since the price rise in October 2023, the nationwide JR Pass only pays off if you're covering a lot of distance in one week, roughly Tokyo to Kyoto and on to Hiroshima or further, then back. Most first-time visitors save more with individual shinkansen tickets plus a cheaper regional pass for the area where they spend the most time. And in Tokyo, the Tokyo Subway Ticket is the best value of all.

Below is how to check the math for your own route, followed by the passes that most often make sense, grouped by area.

Do the math before you buy anything

A 7-day ordinary JR Pass costs ¥50,000 (on Klook it showed as about US$335 at the time of writing). Compare that with what your main long-distance trips would cost as single tickets. A reserved seat on the shinkansen between Tokyo and Kyoto is about ¥14,000 each way.

Short local rides in Kyoto or Osaka don't change this much. The long shinkansen legs decide it. One more catch: the JR Pass doesn't cover the fastest Nozomi and Mizuho trains without paying an extra fee. That's fine on most routes, since the slightly slower Hikari trains run frequently.

Tokyo

  • Tokyo Subway Ticket (24, 48 or 72 hours). Unlimited rides on all Tokyo Metro and Toei subway lines, from about US$6 on Klook. It's rated 4.8 from almost 90,000 reviews, and for good reason. Just two or three rides a day already makes it worth it. It doesn't cover JR lines such as the Yamanote Line, so keep an IC card for those. Tokyo Subway Ticket on Klook (affiliate link).
  • JR Tokyo Wide Pass (3 days). Worth it if you're doing at least two big day trips from Tokyo in three days, for example Nikko plus Karuizawa, or the Mt. Fuji area. From about US$104 at the time of writing. JR Tokyo Wide Pass on Klook (affiliate link). Check the coverage map against your day trips first, because not every operator around Fuji is JR.

Arriving at the airport? See our Narita and Haneda guides for the best ticket into the city.

Hakone and Mt. Fuji

  • Hakone Freepass (2 or 3 days). Covers the classic Hakone loop: the mountain railway, cable car, ropeway, the pirate ship on Lake Ashi and most local buses, plus the Odakyu train from Shinjuku. If you're doing the full loop, it's almost always cheaper than paying for each ride. From about US$45 on Klook. The Romancecar express needs a small extra ticket. Hakone Freepass on Klook (affiliate link).

Kyoto and Osaka (Kansai)

  • JR Kansai Area Pass (1 to 4 days). Covers JR trains around Kyoto, Osaka, Nara, Kobe and Himeji, plus the Haruka express to Kansai Airport. From about US$17 at the time of writing. The best value is on days when you use the Haruka or go out to Nara or Himeji. In Kyoto itself, many sights are better reached by bus or private railway, which this pass doesn't cover. JR Kansai Area Pass on Klook (affiliate link).
  • JR Kansai Wide Area Pass (5 days). Adds the shinkansen between Shin-Osaka and Okayama, plus longer trips such as Kinosaki Onsen and Shirahama. Good if you're adding Himeji by shinkansen and an onsen trip. From about US$75. JR Kansai Wide Area Pass on Klook (affiliate link).

Kanazawa, Takayama and the Japan Alps

  • JR Hokuriku Arch Pass (7 days). Made for the popular "arch" route: Tokyo → Kanazawa on the Hokuriku shinkansen, then on to Kyoto and Osaka, in either direction. If that's your route, this is often the best pass in Japan for the money. From about US$221 at the time of writing. JR Hokuriku Arch Pass on Klook (affiliate link).
  • JR Takayama-Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass (5 days). For a loop from Osaka or Kyoto through Takayama, Shirakawa-go and Kanazawa. It includes some highway buses on that route. Check the current coverage and reserve bus seats early, since they fill up in winter. From about US$125. Takayama-Hokuriku Area Pass on Klook (affiliate link).
  • JR East passes. If you're heading north or into the mountains from Tokyo (Nagano for the snow monkeys, Niigata, or Tohoku), one of the JR East passes can beat single tickets. From about US$221. JR East Pass on Klook (affiliate link). Pick the area version that matches your route.

Nationwide: the JR Pass

The Whole Japan Rail Pass (7, 14 or 21 days) still makes sense for one kind of trip: lots of long shinkansen rides packed into a short window. Think Tokyo, Kanazawa, Kyoto, Hiroshima and back in one week. If that's you, it also saves you from buying tickets one by one. JR Pass on Klook (affiliate link).

A few things railway staff wish visitors knew

  • You still want an IC card. Passes rarely cover everything (subways, buses, private railways). An IC card on your phone covers the gaps. See our IC card guide.
  • Reserve shinkansen seats, especially at peak times. With most passes, seat reservations are free, but unreserved cars fill up fast around holidays.
  • Big suitcases on the shinkansen. Bags over 160 cm (length + width + height) need a reserved oversized-luggage seat on the Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu shinkansen. See our shinkansen guide, or send your suitcase ahead instead.
  • Check the activation rules. Most passes run on consecutive days from the first day you use them, so plan your biggest travel days together.
