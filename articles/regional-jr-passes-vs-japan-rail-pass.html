Regional JR Passes vs the Japan Rail Pass: Which One Fits Your Route

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: If your trip stays inside one JR company's region — Kansai, Hokuriku, eastern Japan or Kyushu — a regional pass costs less than the nationwide Japan Rail Pass. The nationwide pass starts to make sense when you need the Tokaido Shinkansen between Tokyo and Osaka, because none of the regional passes in this guide cover it, and when you are combining several regions in one trip.

Japan's railways are run by several JR companies, and each sells its own passes for visitors. The nationwide pass works across all of them; the regional passes only work in one company's area, but at a much lower price. Here is how they compare at the time of writing (November 2026), using the operators' own pages. Prices can change, so check the official page before you buy.

Two changes to know before you read older guides

  • The JR West Kansai Area Pass is gone. JR West's page says: "Sales ended as of September 30, 2026." Passes bought by then remain valid for their validity period. For a budget option in the Osaka–Kyoto area, JR West still sells the Kansai Mini Pass (below).
  • The old JR East Pass (Tohoku area) is no longer sold. JR East stopped selling the Tohoku-area and Nagano/Niigata-area passes on 13 March 2026. Its new JR EAST PASS covers "all JR East lines and some lines operated by other companies."

Also, nationwide Japan Rail Pass prices now depend on where you buy. For purchases made on or after 1 October 2026, overseas travel agencies charge more than the official website. The JR Group says the online prices "will remain unchanged for a limited time."

Kansai: Osaka, Kyoto, Nara and further out

For a trip based in Osaka and Kyoto, start with the Kansai Mini Pass: ¥3,000 for 3 consecutive days. It is a budget pass with limits — JR West says the Haruka airport express needs a separate ticket and shinkansen cannot be used. It is sold as an e-ticket from sales outlets outside Japan, which you exchange within 3 months of issue.

If you are going further, the Kansai WIDE Area Pass (¥12,000, 5 days) adds reserved seats on the Sanyo Shinkansen between Shin-Osaka and Okayama, and on limited expresses such as the Haruka, Kuroshio, Konotori and Super Hakuto. It does not cover the Tokaido Shinkansen, the Sanyo Shinkansen beyond Okayama to Hakata, or expressway buses. It is worth pricing out if your days use those trains — for example a trip to Okayama, the Konotori or Kuroshio limited expresses, or the Haruka to the airport. See our guide to onsen towns by train from Osaka and Kyoto and Kansai Airport to Osaka and Kyoto.

JR West's Kansai WIDE Area Pass page says "Product sales prices are set according to the date of purchase and not the date of exchange or use." You can buy it through an overseas agency, through JR-WEST Online Train Reservation, or from a travel agency in Japan. If you'd rather have it as an e-voucher before you fly, the Kansai Wide Area Pass on Klook (affiliate link) was rated 4.8 from about 10,800 reviews and showed from US$75.09 on Klook at the time of writing; prices vary by date and option.

Hokuriku: Kanazawa, Toyama and Fukui

Hokuriku has three JR West options, and which one fits depends on where you enter the region.

  • Coming from Osaka or Kyoto: the Kansai–Hokuriku Area Pass (¥19,000, 7 days) covers reserved seats on the Thunderbird limited express, the Hokuriku Shinkansen between Tsuruga and Joetsu-Myoko, and the Sanyo Shinkansen between Shin-Osaka and Okayama. It does not cover the Super Hakuto or Super Inaba, the Hokuriku Shinkansen between Joetsu-Myoko and Tokyo, or the Tokaido Shinkansen.
  • Coming from Tokyo and leaving via Osaka (or the other way round): the Hokuriku Arch Pass (¥35,000, 7 days) covers reserved and non-reserved seats on the Hokuriku Shinkansen all the way between Tsuruga and Tokyo, plus the Thunderbird and other limited expresses. It does not cover the Tokaido or Sanyo Shinkansen, and GranClass or Green Car seats need separate tickets.
  • Already in Hokuriku: the Hokuriku Area Pass (¥7,000, 4 days) covers non-reserved seats only on the Hokuriku Shinkansen between Tsuruga and Kurobe-Unazukionsen.

For what to see once you're there, our guide to historic towns you can reach by JR includes Kanazawa.

Eastern Japan and Tohoku: the new JR EAST PASS

The JR EAST PASS costs ¥35,000 for 5 days or ¥50,000 for 10 days. JR East says it is valid for 5 or 10 consecutive days, so it is not a flexible pass where you pick separate days. It covers JR East lines, including reserved seats in ordinary cars on limited expresses and shinkansen, plus some other companies' lines, including the Tokyo Monorail, Aoimori Railway, Iwate Galaxy Railway, Sendai Airport Transit, Sanriku Railway, Hokuetsu Express and Tobu through-trains to Nikko and Kinugawa.

It does not cover the Tokaido Shinkansen, Izu Kyuko or Fujikyu, and "A separate fee is applicable if you wish to use the Green Car or GranClass." JR East also says: "While visiting Japan, you can only use one JR EAST PASS at a time."

Compared with the nationwide pass, the 5-day JR EAST PASS is ¥15,000 cheaper than the 7-day nationwide pass bought on the official website. If your trip is Tokyo plus Tohoku, Nikko or Niigata, and you don't need to go west of Tokyo by shinkansen, the JR EAST PASS is worth comparing first. See our Nikko day trip guide for one route it covers.

Kyushu: three sizes of pass

JR Kyushu sells three versions, so match the pass to how far south you go. The All Kyushu pass covers the Kyushu Shinkansen from Hakata to Kagoshima-Chuo and the Nishi Kyushu Shinkansen (Takeo-Onsen–Nagasaki). The Northern Kyushu pass covers the Kyushu Shinkansen Hakata–Kumamoto and the Nishi Kyushu Shinkansen; the Southern Kyushu pass covers Kumamoto–Kagoshima-Chuo but not the Kamome to Nagasaki.

Seat reservations with the pass are limited on the smaller passes. JR Kyushu lists them as "All Kyushu：Unlimited", "Northern Kyushu：Up to 6 times" and "Southern Kyushu：Up to 6 times". Reservations made in advance through Online Booking don't count toward those limits, but JR Kyushu's FAQ says "There is a charge for reserved seat tickets that are booked through Online Booking."

You can buy the pass through JR Kyushu's Online Booking (credit card only, and virtual cards are not accepted), through overseas travel agencies, or at designated JR Kyushu counters after you arrive. You need your original passport; photos or copies are not accepted.

When the nationwide Japan Rail Pass is the better choice

The nationwide pass costs ¥50,000 for 7 days in Ordinary class on the official website. It is most likely to pay off on trips that link regions — for example Tokyo to Kyoto and Osaka on the Tokaido Shinkansen, then on to Hiroshima or Kyushu. None of the regional passes above cover the Tokaido Shinkansen.

Two rules matter here:

  • Nozomi and Mizuho need an extra ticket. The pass is valid on these trains "Only when an [ONLY WITH JAPAN RAIL PASS] NOZOMI MIZUHO Ticket is purchased separately before boarding the train". Examples: Tokyo–Kyoto or Tokyo–Shin-Osaka ¥4,960, Tokyo–Hiroshima ¥6,500, Shin-Osaka–Hakata ¥4,960. Otherwise, use the Hikari, Sakura, Kodama or Tsubame.
  • A pass is not a seat. The official conditions say "The PASS itself does not guarantee you a seat", and "If you want to use a reserved seat, be sure to book a reserved seat (no additional payment needed)." Reserved seats can be booked in advance only if you bought the pass on the official website.

A quick check: look up the regular fares for your planned journeys on the operators' booking sites, add them up, and compare the total with the pass price.

Who can use these passes

These passes are aimed at overseas visitors, and the eligibility wording differs by company:

  • Japan Rail Pass: "You are a foreign tourist visiting Japan from abroad for sight-seeing, under the entry status of "temporary visitor."" (There is a separate category for some Japanese nationals living abroad, with its own conditions.)
  • JR West passes: you must hold "a passport issued by a foreign government" and be "entering in Japan as a "短期滞在(Temporary Visitor)"." JR West also says individual customers may not buy or exchange more than one of the same pass for the same period.
  • JR EAST PASS: for "Customers with a non-Japanese passport". You cannot receive a pass with only a copy of your passport.
  • JR Kyushu Rail Pass: "Non-Japanese citizens who are visiting Japan for the purpose of sightseeing and traveling on a temporary visitor visa."

Whichever pass you choose, you will still want an IC card for subways, buses and private railways that JR passes don't cover — see our IC card guide — and our shinkansen guide for how boarding and luggage work.
