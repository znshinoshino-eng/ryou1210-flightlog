Night Buses Between Tokyo and Osaka or Kyoto: Are They Worth It?

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: a night bus is worth it if saving money matters more than sleep. The cheapest seats cost a fraction of a shinkansen ticket, and you arrive early in the morning without paying for a hotel that night. But the fastest shinkansen does Tokyo to Shin-Osaka in about 2 hours 21 minutes, while the bus takes all night. If you only have a week in Japan, or you sleep badly sitting up, the train is usually the better choice.

The details below come from the operators' own websites: West Japan JR Bus and JR Bus Kanto (who run the "Dream" night buses), their booking site Japan Expressway Bus Net, WILLER EXPRESS, the Shinjuku Expressway Bus Terminal, and JR Central for the shinkansen.

Who runs the night buses

Many companies run overnight buses between Tokyo and the Kansai area. Two names you'll see most often:

  • JR buses ("Dream" buses). Run by JR Bus Kanto and West Japan JR Bus. They're booked on Japan Expressway Bus Net (Kousoku Bus Net in Japanese), the booking site run by the JR bus companies, which has an English version. You can also buy tickets at JR highway bus ticket counters.
  • WILLER EXPRESS. A private operator with its own English booking site. You can pick your seat on buses WILLER EXPRESS runs itself.

The JR night buses stop at Busta Shinjuku and Tokyo Station at the Tokyo end, and at Kyoto Station and the JR Expressway Bus Terminal at Osaka Station in Kansai. Some trips also serve Tokyo Disney Resort, Universal Studios Japan, Nara or Kobe. Check the exact stops and times for your date when you book, because some trips only run on certain days.

Seat types and fares on the JR "Dream" buses

The seat matters more than anything else on a night bus. West Japan JR Bus lists these options for its Tokyo–Kyoto/Osaka/Kobe buses. Fares are the operator's published prices at the time of writing (October 2026) and change with the date.

Women-only seats are offered on Gran Dream and Seishun Eco Dream. Book them as a separate seat type. West Japan JR Bus says toilets, free Wi-Fi and power outlets (or USB ports) are on its buses except on some routes, so check the icons on the booking page.

Tickets for the JR buses go on sale at 10:00, one month before the travel date (counted to the day before, so travel on May 1 opens on March 30). Weekend and holiday buses fill up first.

A night bus makes the most sense when:

  • You're on a tight budget and you'd otherwise pay for a hotel that night.
  • You travel light, with a backpack or a small case.
  • You can sleep sitting up, or you can pay for a 3-across or 2-across seat.

Take the shinkansen if you're on a short trip and can't afford a tired first day in Kyoto, if you carry a large suitcase, or if you have a JR Pass. The Japan Rail Pass is not valid on JR Bus express bus routes, so the "Dream" buses aren't free with it. Our shinkansen guide explains tickets, seats and luggage. If you'd rather book the train ahead in English, Klook sells a Tokyo to Osaka shinkansen ticket (affiliate link) and a Tokyo to Kyoto shinkansen ticket (affiliate link). On Klook at the time of writing, these were from about US$88 and US$85. Compare with smartEX before you buy.

Luggage: the biggest catch

Bus luggage space is limited, and the rules differ by operator.

  • JR buses (West Japan JR Bus): one item per passenger in the luggage compartment, with length + width + height within 1.5 m. Large travel suitcases, skis, snowboards and bicycles (including folding ones) are listed as items that may be refused. Bags you keep at your seat must be within 1 m in total dimensions.
  • WILLER EXPRESS buses: up to 2 pieces in the trunk. One piece can be up to 155 cm in total dimensions, and two pieces up to 240 cm combined. Carry-on must be under 10 kg in total and fit on the rack or under your seat. Some WILLER double-decker buses have no trunk at all, so check your booking confirmation.
  • Don't put valuables, fragile items or power banks in the trunk. Keep power banks with you at your seat.

If you have a large suitcase, the simplest fix is to send it ahead to your next hotel. Our luggage forwarding guide explains how.

Boarding at Busta Shinjuku

Busta Shinjuku (the Shinjuku Expressway Bus Terminal) is directly connected to the JR New South Gate of Shinjuku Station, about a 2-minute walk. The New South Gate is open from 5:45 to 24:00. Outside those hours, use the South Gate, about 4 minutes away. Night buses leave from the 4th floor, and arriving buses use the 3rd floor.

  • The waiting room is open from 3:30 to 25:00 (1 a.m.) and has an ATM and a convenience store.
  • Coin lockers on the 4th floor can be used for up to 72 hours.
  • There is free Wi-Fi, and the information counter offers English support.

Our Shinjuku Station guide shows how to find your way through the station. In Osaka, West Japan JR Bus says its JR Expressway Bus Terminal is about 2 minutes from the Central gate of JR Osaka Station. At Kyoto Station, JR buses use the stops just outside the station on the Karasuma (Central Exit) side. See our Kyoto and Osaka transport guide for the next leg of your trip.

Rules to know before you book

  • Be at the stop early. West Japan JR Bus asks passengers to arrive about 10 minutes before departure with their ticket or web boarding pass ready.
  • Miss the bus and the ticket is gone. On Japan Expressway Bus Net, a ticket becomes invalid if you miss the bus, and it can't be changed or refunded after departure.
  • Changes mean cancelling. Online changes and refunds on Japan Expressway Bus Net close 2 hours before the bus leaves its first stop on most routes, and a cancellation fee applies. WILLER's cancellation fees depend on the plan, and its own reservation page shows them. WILLER also charges a ¥330 arrangement fee per trip on many reserved-seat routes, which isn't refunded.
  • Delays aren't refunded. If traffic or weather makes the bus late, the operators don't refund the fare or pay for connections or hotels. If the operator cancels the bus, you get a full refund.
  • On board: keep your seatbelt on while the bus is moving, set your phone to silent and don't make calls.

Finally, plan your arrival morning. Buses can reach Kyoto or Osaka before shops and hotel check-in open. Know where you'll store your bag and where you'll get breakfast. For getting around once you arrive, an IC card helps a lot. See our IC card guide.
