Shirakawa-go by Bus from Takayama and Kanazawa

Sightseeing guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: there is no train to Shirakawa-go. You take a highway bus: about 50 minutes from Takayama, or about 1 hour 15 minutes from Kanazawa. The Kanazawa buses need a reservation, and some Takayama buses do too, so book those seats online before the day. Give the village 2 to 3 hours on foot. In winter, wear boots and leave slack in your plans, because snow and parking queues can delay the buses by two hours or more.

Shirakawa-go is the village of steep thatched farmhouses in the mountains of Gifu Prefecture. Together with Gokayama it was registered as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995, as the "Historic Villages of Shirakawa-go and Gokayama". It sits between Takayama and Kanazawa, so most visitors stop there on the way from one town to the other, or go there and back from either one.

The buses: who runs them and what they cost

The route runs Takayama – Shirakawa-go – Kanazawa. It is run by Nohi Bus and Hokutetsu (Hokuriku Railroad), and Nohi's English timetable covers the whole line. Nohi says there are 16 round-trip services a day, "some services are on a reservation basis".

Fares change on 1 December 2026. These are adult one-way fares from that date, as published by Nohi Bus at the time of writing (November 2026):

Until 30 November 2026 the fares are ¥2,800, ¥2,800 and ¥4,200, and Hokutetsu's own page may still show the old ¥2,800 from Kanazawa. Nohi lists one-way fares only, with no return fare, so a round trip is two one-way tickets. Nohi says one child under 6 rides free with each accompanying passenger, but "if child occupies a seat, a child fare will be charged", and if the bus is full a free child may be turned away. The Kanazawa departure is from the West Gate side of Kanazawa Station, so check you're on the right side of the station.

Journey times are the operators' own figures. The Shirakawa-go Tourist Association gives about 1 hour 20 minutes from Kanazawa.

Booking your seat

On Nohi's timetable, departures marked R are "Reservation required". The rest are non-reserved, and for those Nohi sells mobile tickets. Hokutetsu marks its Kanazawa highway buses "Reservations required" and asks you to "please make sure to reserve seats in advance". If you're coming from Kanazawa, book.

  • Online: both operators sell seats through Japan Bus Online in English. Hokutetsu says online booking is open 24 hours a day, every day of the year.
  • By phone: Nohi Bus Reservation Center, (0577) 32-1688, 9:00–17:00; Hokutetsu (Kanazawa line), (076) 234-0123, 9:00–18:00 according to Nohi's page.
  • On the day: Nohi sells tickets until 15 minutes before departure, but it advises buying early.

Check the current timetable on Nohi Bus's English page before you book, because the December 2026 revision also changes the times. Look at the last bus back too, and book it.

On a rail-pass trip, JR West's 2024 announcement of its Takayama-Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass listed these highway buses, and the World Heritage Bus via Gokayama, as covered. Check the current pass terms before you rely on it. We cover it in our historic towns guide, alongside the trains to Takayama and Kanazawa. For other passes, see the rail pass guide.

Luggage: send the big case ahead

The bus has an underfloor trunk, but Nohi warns: "The trunk is shared by many passengers. Due to limited space, we may be unable to accommodate large items." Shirakawa Village's own manners guide is headed "Backpacks Work Better Than Suitcases!" If you're moving between Takayama and Kanazawa, send the suitcase ahead with a delivery service (see our luggage forwarding guide) and travel with a day bag.

Nohi also asks you to "bring your mobile battery with you into the bus cabin" and not to store it in the luggage bay. Keep power banks in your hand luggage.

If you do arrive with bags, the Shirakawa-go Bus Terminal has coin lockers (08:00–17:00), at ¥500 for small and medium and ¥1,000 for large, per use. A luggage storage service right next to the terminal is open until 17:00. Both close at 17:00, so collect your bags before then. Our coin locker guide explains how the lockers work.

Timing a day trip

The village starts about a minute's walk from the bus terminal. The Tourist Association says it takes "about 2-3 hours to walk around the village", and there's no sightseeing bus around the village, so you explore on foot. Walking around is free. Individual houses and facilities charge their own admission.

  • Wada House: 9:00–17:00, closed on irregular days. Adults ¥400, primary school children ¥200, cash only.
  • Kanda House: 9:00–17:00, closed on Wednesdays, adults ¥400 / children ¥200 (the village notes its listing is "for reference").
  • Observatory shuttle to the viewpoint above the village: every 20 minutes from 10:00 to 14:40, with a pause at noon, from in front of Wada House. ¥200 one way, cash only. The stop is about 5 minutes' walk from the bus terminal.

The shuttle's last run is at 14:40. If you want the classic view from above, go up first and walk the village afterwards. Pick your buses from the current timetable with that in mind, remembering the shuttle's break at noon.

Leave slack, though. Nohi warns that "Delays of two hours or more can occur due to traffic from waiting for private car parking at Shirakawa-go, heavy snowfall, or expressway closures". Don't plan a tight train connection in Takayama or Kanazawa right after the bus.

A few local rules are worth knowing. The farmhouses are people's homes. The village asks visitors not to "enter private premises or fields, open doors, or peek through windows without express permission." There are no trash cans in the village, so carry your rubbish out. The village also asks you to smoke only in designated areas.

Winter in Shirakawa-go

The village says "Snow falls annually in Shirakawa-go from December to March", and the Tourist Association says snow keeps building up from late December to late February. Snow on the thatched roofs is the classic picture, but the paths get slippery. The village warns there are "frequent accidents caused by slipping on snow and ice, even when snow is not actually falling" and asks you to "refrain from wearing heels" and use "appropriate winter footwear such as snowshoes or boots". It recommends rainwear or a poncho over an umbrella in snow.

The winter light-up (January 2027)

On a few evenings each winter the farmhouses are lit up after dark. The 41st light-up is planned for 11 January (a public holiday), and 17, 24 and 31 January 2027 (Sundays), 17:30–19:30. Entry is by advance reservation only. The official notice says "Visitors without a reservation will not be admitted" and "Admission by route bus, private car, rental car, taxi, and chartered buses also requires advance reservation." Arriving on the regular bus doesn't get you in on those nights unless you have a reservation. The event "may be cancelled if severe nationwide cold weather is expected to cause major transportation disruptions". Check the Shirakawa-go Tourist Association site for how to book.

Quick checklist

  • Book reserved buses online, both ways, before the day, and always from Kanazawa.
  • From 1 December 2026: ¥2,900 one way from Takayama or Kanazawa.
  • Kanazawa buses leave from the West Gate, Platform 4.
  • Send big suitcases ahead; lockers at the terminal close at 17:00.
  • Do the observatory shuttle before 14:40, then walk the village (2–3 hours).
  • Carry cash for houses and the shuttle, and keep power banks out of the trunk. Our eSIM and power bank guide has more on power banks.
  • In winter: boots, no heels, and leave room for delays.
