Historic Towns You Can Reach by JR: Kanazawa, Takayama, Kurashiki and Kawagoe

Sightseeing guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: you do not need a car to see old Japan. Kanazawa is about two and a half hours from Tokyo on the Hokuriku Shinkansen, Takayama is about two hours and twenty minutes from Nagoya on the Limited Express Hida, Kurashiki is about 75 minutes from Shin-Osaka with one easy change, and Kawagoe is about 50 minutes from Shinjuku on an ordinary JR train. All four are walkable from the station.

Each one kept its old streets while the cities around it modernised — a castle town, a mountain merchant town, a canal town and a warehouse town. Because all four sit on JR lines, they fit neatly into a rail pass trip. Below is the train for each town, what to see when you step off it, and which pass makes the trip cheaper.

Kanazawa: the castle town on the shinkansen

The fastest Kagayaki on the Hokuriku Shinkansen covers Tokyo to Kanazawa in about 2 hours 30 minutes — JR West gives 2 hours 28 minutes as the fastest time — with no change of train. Kagayaki trains are all reserved seats, so book a seat before you board; the slower Hakutaka takes about three hours and has non-reserved cars. From Kyoto or Osaka, ride the Thunderbird to Tsuruga and change to the shinkansen there.

Start at Kenrokuen, one of Japan's three great landscape gardens, right beside Kanazawa Castle. Built up over nearly two centuries by the Maeda lords who ruled the Kaga domain, it is open 7:00 to 18:00 from 1 March to 15 October and 8:00 to 17:00 from 16 October to the end of February, and entry is ¥320 for adults at the time of writing. Go early: the garden opens free of charge before its paid hours begin, and the morning light on Kasumigaike Pond and the two-legged Kotoji-toro lantern is the best view in town.

Then take the Kanazawa Loop Bus to the Higashi Chaya District, the largest of Kanazawa's three historic geisha districts — a five-minute walk from the Hashibacho bus stop. The wooden teahouses here date back to the 1820s, the streets are a designated cultural asset, and geisha still entertain here today. Two teahouses, Shima and Kaikaro, are open to the public. Kanazawa produces 99% of Japan's gold leaf — expect gold-leaf ice cream and crafts in the shops.

Takayama: the old town in the mountains

The train ride to Takayama is half the experience. From Nagoya, the Limited Express Hida runs up the Hida River valley to Takayama in about 2 hours 20 minutes, according to JR Central. From Tokyo, take the Tokaido Shinkansen to Nagoya and change — about four hours in all. The Hida is a reserved-seat train in busy periods, and from 25 December 2026 to 5 January 2027 all its seats are reserved only, so reserve before boarding then.

The old town is a ten-minute walk from Takayama Station. Sanmachi Suji is a row of dark-wood merchant houses from the Edo period, now holding sake breweries, craft shops and cafes. The morning markets — one by the river, one in front of Takayama Jinya, the old government house — are where locals still shop, and they are at their best before 10 in the morning.

Takayama also works as a base: the bus to Shirakawa-go, the village of steep thatched-roof farmhouses, leaves from beside the station, and the Takayama-Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass (below) covers that bus. Do not try it as a rushed day trip from Tokyo, though — the four hours each way eats the day. Stay a night and walk the old town after the day-trippers have left.

Kurashiki: the canal town near Okayama

Kurashiki is the easiest town on this list to add to a Kansai trip. From Shin-Osaka, take the Sanyo Shinkansen to Okayama — 45 to 50 minutes on a Sakura, which the Japan Rail Pass covers — then change to a local train on the JR Sanyo Line for the 17-minute ride to Kurashiki. The whole trip takes about 75 minutes and costs ¥5,940 one way with an unreserved seat at the time of writing. Buy one ticket through to Kurashiki, not Shin-Kurashiki eight minutes further west.

The reason to come is the Bikan Historical Quarter, a 10- to 15-minute walk south of Kurashiki Station. White-walled storehouses from the Edo period line a willow-edged canal, and small wooden boats still carry visitors along it. The old rice warehouses are now museums, cafes and craft shops. The Ohara Museum of Art, Japan's first museum of Western art, stands on the canal; it closes on Mondays, so plan around that. A JR Kansai Wide Area Pass (¥12,000 for five days at the time of writing) covers both legs.

Kawagoe: "Little Edo" an hour from Tokyo

Kawagoe is the only town on this list you can reach on an ordinary commuter train, and that is its charm: no shinkansen, no reservation, just a JR line out of Shinjuku. The JR Saikyo Line rapid runs through to the Kawagoe Line and reaches Kawagoe Station in about 50 minutes for ¥810 at the time of writing (¥803 by IC card). From Ikebukuro, the Tobu Tojo Line express is faster (about 30 minutes) and cheaper (¥490) — JR is the better choice from Shinjuku or Shibuya.

Kawagoe's nickname is "Little Edo", and the warehouse district earns it. Kurazukuri Street is lined with clay-walled storehouses built after a great fire in 1893, and the wooden Toki no Kane bell tower — the symbol of the town — still rings four times a day. Note that Kawagoe Station is more than 20 minutes on foot from Kurazukuri Street, so take the loop bus from the station's east exit. A half day covers the warehouse street, the bell tower, Kita-in Temple and a sweet-potato snack.

Which pass covers these towns?

Single tickets work fine for one town, but if you are visiting two or more, a pass usually wins:

Osaka–Takayama–Shirakawa-go–Kanazawa: take the Takayama-Hokuriku pass. Our rail pass guide works through the maths.

A few tips before you go

  • Reserve the long legs. The Kagayaki is all reserved seats, and the Hida is reserved-only in the New Year period. Reservations are free with a pass.
  • Stay overnight in Kanazawa and Takayama; Kurashiki and Kawagoe are easy day trips.
  • Send your suitcase ahead. The old streets in all four towns are best walked with empty hands. See our guide to luggage forwarding in Japan.
  • Carry an IC card. The local buses in Kanazawa and Kawagoe, and the short local train to Kurashiki, all take Suica, ICOCA and the other nationwide cards. See our IC card guide.
  • Check the date. Timetables change every March, and pass prices changed in March 2026 — the Arch Pass rose from ¥30,000 to ¥35,000. Confirm times and prices on the JR sites before you travel.

Four towns, four kinds of old Japan, no car needed. Pick the one nearest your route — or string two together on one of the passes above.
