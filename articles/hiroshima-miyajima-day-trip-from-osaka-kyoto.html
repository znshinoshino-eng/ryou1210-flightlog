Hiroshima and Miyajima from Osaka or Kyoto by Shinkansen

Day-trip guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: yes, you can do Hiroshima and Miyajima in one long day from Osaka or Kyoto. The shinkansen from Shin-Osaka to Hiroshima takes roughly an hour and a half. In Hiroshima, take the streetcar to the Peace Memorial Park and museum first. Then head to Miyajima, either by the direct boat from the park (about 45 minutes) or by JR train to Miyajimaguchi plus a 10-minute ferry. Start early: the museum gets very crowded in the middle of the day.

The times and fees below come from the operators' own websites: Hiroshima Electric Railway (Hiroden), JR West Miyajima Ferry, Aqua Net Hiroshima, the Peace Memorial Museum and Itsukushima Shrine, plus Japan's national tourism organization (JNTO).

Getting to Hiroshima by shinkansen

Hiroshima is on the Sanyo Shinkansen, the line that continues west from Shin-Osaka towards Fukuoka. JNTO puts the Osaka–Hiroshima ride at about 80 to 90 minutes on the fastest trains. From Kyoto, you board at Kyoto Station, and the train stops at Shin-Osaka on the way, so allow a little longer.

  • Which train? Nozomi and Mizuho are the fastest. Hikari and Sakura stop a few more times. Kodama trains stop everywhere and are much slower, so avoid them for a day trip.
  • Reserve a seat for the morning run if you can, and book your return train before you leave Hiroshima, especially on weekends and holidays.
  • Big suitcase? Bags over 160 cm in total dimensions need a reserved oversized-luggage seat on this line. For a day trip, just leave your bag at the hotel. Our shinkansen guide explains seat types, luggage and how to board.

Fares change by season and how you book, so check the current price on JR's booking sites (smartEX or JR West's online reservation) when you plan your day.

Hiroshima Station to Peace Memorial Park

The easiest way is the Hiroden streetcar from outside the station's South Exit. Take Line 2 or Line 6 and get off at Genbaku Dome-mae (A-Bomb Dome). Hiroden says the ride takes about 13 minutes. The A-Bomb Dome is right by the stop, and the park and museum are a short walk across the river.

At the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum, plan on spending at least an hour and a half. Key details from the museum's website at the time of writing (October 2026):

The museum itself says it is "very crowded in the daytime" and recommends buying a web ticket in advance, because buying on the day can take a while. In the evening slots, only people with a web ticket can enter. If you're coming on an early shinkansen, going straight to the museum is the best way to beat the crowds.

Getting to Miyajima: three ways

Miyajima (Itsukushima) is an island, so the trip always ends with a boat. Here are the main options:

RouteTime (approx.)Good for
Direct boat from Motoyasu Pier, next to Peace Park (Aqua Net Hiroshima)45 minGoing straight from the park, no transfers
JR Sanyo Line from Hiroshima Station to Miyajimaguchi, then ferryAbout 30 min by train, 5 min walk, 10 min ferryJR Pass holders
Hiroden streetcar Line 2 to Miyajima-guchi, then ferryAbout 49 min from Genbaku Dome-mae, plus the ferryIf you're already on the streetcar and not in a hurry
  • Direct boat: Aqua Net's World Heritage Sea Route leaves from Motoyasu Pier, about 150 m south of the A-Bomb Dome. At the time of writing, it costs ¥2,400 one way or ¥4,400 return for adults (12 and over). You can book online up to the day before, and buy tickets at the ticket office on the day itself. Sailings can be cancelled or changed when the tide is very high or very low.
  • JR route: from Hiroshima Station, take the JR Sanyo Line from platform 1. It's about 30 minutes to Miyajimaguchi, then about 5 minutes on foot to the pier. The JR Miyajima Ferry crosses in about 10 minutes, and departures run about every 15 minutes during the day. The ferry accepts IC cards.
  • Two ferry companies run from Miyajimaguchi: JR West Miyajima Ferry and Miyajima Matsudai Kisen. Only the JR one is covered by the JR Pass. Between 9:10 and 16:10, JR's "Great Torii" sailings take a route that passes close to the torii gate, which is worth catching on the way over.

Miyajima Visitor Tax: everyone entering the island pays ¥100 per visit, on top of the boat fare (preschool children are exempt).

On Miyajima: Itsukushima Shrine

From the ferry pier, it's an easy walk along the waterfront to Itsukushima Shrine, the famous shrine built over the water with the great torii in front. Opening hours from the shrine's official website:

Admission is ¥300 for adults, ¥200 for high school students and ¥100 for elementary and junior high students. A combined ticket with the Treasure Hall is ¥500 for adults, sold only at the shrine entrance. The corridors have small gaps between the floorboards, so the shrine suggests comfortable shoes rather than heels.

In autumn and winter, the shrine closes earlier in the afternoon, so don't leave Miyajima until the end of the day if the shrine is your top priority. Go to Miyajima first after Peace Park, not the other way around.

Does the JR Pass help for this trip?

The JR Pass covers the Sanyo Shinkansen on Hikari, Sakura and Kodama trains, the JR Sanyo Line to Miyajimaguchi and the JR Miyajima Ferry. It does not cover Nozomi or Mizuho trains unless you buy a separate "NOZOMI MIZUHO Ticket" for that ride. It also doesn't cover the Hiroden streetcar or the Aqua Net boat.

At the time of writing, a 7-day ordinary JR Pass costs ¥50,000 when bought on the official online site. A single Osaka–Hiroshima round trip won't come close to that on its own. But if Hiroshima is one stop on a longer trip, for example Tokyo to Kyoto and then on to Hiroshima within a week, the pass may pay off. Our rail pass guide shows how to do the math. If it fits your route, you can buy one through JR Pass on Klook (affiliate link).

JR West also sells its own JR-WEST RAIL PASS range for overseas visitors, including a Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass, and the JR Miyajima Ferry accepts these too. Compare the current price and coverage on JR West's website with the cost of single tickets for your dates.

A simple day plan

  1. Take an early shinkansen from Shin-Osaka or Kyoto to Hiroshima.
  2. Ride the Hiroden streetcar (Line 2 or 6) to Genbaku Dome-mae. See the A-Bomb Dome, then visit the Peace Memorial Museum with a web ticket booked ahead.
  3. Walk to Motoyasu Pier for the direct boat to Miyajima, or go back to Hiroshima Station for the JR Sanyo Line and ferry.
  4. Visit Itsukushima Shrine before it closes, then explore the island's shopping street.
  5. Return by ferry and JR to Hiroshima Station, and catch your reserved shinkansen back.
