Where to Stay in Kyoto on a First Trip: Kyoto Station, Shijo/Kawaramachi or Gion?

Area guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: stay near Kyoto Station if you're arriving with big suitcases, riding the shinkansen or taking day trips to Nara, Fushimi Inari or Arashiyama by JR. Stay around Shijo/Kawaramachi if you want restaurants, shopping and an easy walk to Gion, plus direct Hankyu trains to Osaka. Pick Gion for the old-Kyoto atmosphere in the evening, but only if you'll travel light.

I work on Japan's railways, and Kyoto is different from Tokyo and Osaka in one important way: the city's own tourism office keeps telling visitors to avoid the bus where they can. Kyoto's buses get crowded and stuck in traffic, and drivers can refuse large luggage. So the best Kyoto base is the one that lets you use trains and the subway for most of your trip.

Kyoto Station: best for luggage and day trips

Kyoto Station is the city's rail hub: the Tokaido Shinkansen, JR lines in every direction, the Karasuma subway line and the Kintetsu line to Nara all meet here. It's not the prettiest part of Kyoto, but nothing beats it for moving around.

  • From Tokyo: JR Central says you can reach Kyoto from Tokyo in about 130 minutes by shinkansen. Our shinkansen guide covers tickets and the oversized-luggage rules.
  • Kansai Airport: the JR Haruka limited express runs direct in about 75 minutes, by Kyoto's official tourist information center. The airport limousine bus runs from the Hachijo (south) side; the operator gives about 1 hour 25 minutes to 1 hour 30 minutes to Terminal 1 and an adult fare of ¥2,800 one way at the time of writing. Seats on buses towards the airport need a reservation.
  • Fushimi Inari: the JR Nara Line reaches JR Inari Station in about 9 minutes, and the shrine's big torii gate is right outside.
  • Arashiyama: the JR Sagano Line reaches Saga-Arashiyama in about 11 minutes by rapid train, then it's about an 8-minute walk to the Togetsu-kyo Bridge area.
  • Nara: both JR and Kintetsu run from here. See our Nara day trip guide to pick one.

The catch is the evening: you'll take the subway or a train into Shijo or Gion for dinner. And Kyoto Station is big, with two very different sides. A hotel near the Central Exit (north, by Kyoto Tower) suits city buses and the JR lines; one on the Hachijo side (south) is closer to the shinkansen and the airport buses. Our Kyoto Station guide explains both sides.

Shijo / Kawaramachi: best all-round base for a first trip

Shijo-dori is downtown Kyoto's main street. Its west end is the Karasuma crossing (subway Shijo and Hankyu Karasuma stations); its east end is the Kawaramachi crossing (Hankyu Kyoto-kawaramachi), right by the Kamo River. Nishiki Market, department stores and lots of restaurants are in between.

  • Kyoto Station: the Karasuma subway line takes 4 minutes from Kyoto Station to Shijo, by the Kyoto City Tourism Association. That's your link to the shinkansen and the Haruka.
  • Gion on foot: from the Shijo Kawaramachi crossing, walk east over the river to Yasaka-jinja Shrine. Kyoto's tourism site gives about 13 minutes.
  • Osaka: Hankyu's Kyoto Line runs direct from Kyoto-kawaramachi and Karasuma to Osaka-umeda, with no change. Hankyu's sightseeing train, the Kyo-train Garaku, takes about 43 minutes from Osaka-umeda to Kyoto-kawaramachi, with unreserved seats and no extra charge on top of the normal fare.
  • Arashiyama: take Hankyu to Katsura and change for the Hankyu Arashiyama Line, or follow the route Kyoto's tourism site suggests: Hankyu to Shijo-Omiya, then the Randen (Keifuku) tram to Arashiyama, about 4 minutes on foot from the Togetsu-kyo Bridge.

The downside: Shijo-dori itself is a bus bottleneck. Kyoto's tourism site warns that buses there can take far longer than planned because of traffic. Walk or use the subway and Hankyu, and you'll avoid most of it. If you're staying here after Osaka, our Osaka area guide explains why Umeda is the best match for Hankyu.

Gion: best for atmosphere, worst for suitcases

Gion is the old entertainment district east of the Kamo River, around Yasaka-jinja Shrine and Hanamikoji Street. Staying here means you can walk the Higashiyama area early in the morning and in the evening, when the day-trippers have gone. Kyoto's tourism site recommends walking from Gion to Kiyomizu-dera via Yasaka-jinja, rather than taking the bus.

  • Fushimi Inari: Keihan runs direct from Gion-shijo to Fushimi-Inari Station, about 5 minutes on foot from the shrine. Limited express trains don't stop at Fushimi-Inari, so take a slower train.
  • Osaka: Keihan says its limited express runs between Kyoto and Osaka in about 50 minutes with no transfer.
  • Kyoto Station: by subway, the tourism site's route is the Karasuma Line to Karasuma Oike, then the Tozai Line to Higashiyama, about 15 minutes in all; from there you walk east along Sanjo-dori and south towards Yasaka-jinja. The EX100 sightseeing bus also links Kyoto Station and Gion, but it runs on weekends and holidays only, for ¥500 per adult at the time of writing.

The problem is arrival and departure day. There's no direct train between Gion and Kyoto Station, and a big suitcase on a crowded bus is exactly what the city asks visitors not to do. If you choose Gion, plan to send your bags (see below) or budget for a taxi.

Kyoto's bus and luggage rules, in short

  • Drivers and subway staff can refuse large luggage if they judge it too big or there's too much of it, according to Kyoto City Bus & Subway. They also ask passengers to take backpacks off and hold them.
  • Leave suitcases at the station or hotel before you sightsee. The city points visitors to coin lockers and staffed baggage counters.
  • Same-day hotel delivery: the city's official HANDS FREE KYOTO site says you need to finish the procedure at Kyoto Station by 2 pm for your luggage to reach your accommodation the same day. Some hotels don't accept deliveries, so check first. Our Kyoto Station guide lists the counters, and luggage forwarding covers sending bags between cities.

Booking your hotel

Once you've picked an area, check the walking time from the hotel to the exact station you'll use. "Near Kyoto Station" can mean the Central side or the Hachijo side, and "Shijo" can mean Karasuma or Kawaramachi, a walk of several blocks apart. Tips from the railway side

  • Train first, bus last. Fushimi Inari, Arashiyama and Nara all have rail access from Kyoto Station, and trains don't get stuck in traffic.
  • Splitting your stay? Two nights by Kyoto Station on arrival, then a move to Shijo or Gion with your bags sent ahead, gives you the best of both.
  • Use an IC card. You'll ride JR, the subway, Hankyu, Keihan and Kintetsu. An IC card such as ICOCA or Suica saves buying a ticket each time. For day passes, see getting around Kyoto and Osaka.
