Hokkaido in Winter by Rail: Sapporo and the Snow Festival

Winter rail guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: winter in Hokkaido is a train trip. The Rapid Airport train gets you from New Chitose Airport to Sapporo in about 35 minutes for ¥1,230, and from Sapporo, limited expresses reach Asahikawa in about 90 minutes and Hakodate in about three and a half hours. The one rule to know: every seat on every JR Hokkaido limited express is reserved, so get a reserved-seat ticket first. Making two or more long trips? The Hokkaido Rail Pass (5 days from ¥22,000 bought in advance) usually pays for itself.

Hokkaido is big, and winter driving means snow, ice and short daylight. The trains run through it all, and Sapporo makes a good base: the airport train, the Snow Festival and every limited express all start from there.

New Chitose Airport to Sapporo: take the Rapid Airport train

The JR station sits underneath the domestic terminal, so you walk downstairs from arrivals and you are there. The Rapid Airport runs to Sapporo Station about six times an hour during the day. The fastest, the Special Rapid, takes 33 minutes; a Rapid takes 37 minutes. The adult fare is ¥1,230 for an unreserved seat, and you can just tap an IC card — Suica from Tokyo works, as does Hokkaido's own Kitaca. See our IC card guide if you have not picked one up yet.

If you want a guaranteed seat after a long flight, car 4 is the reserved u-Seat car: ¥2,230 in total. Most trains continue past Sapporo to Otaru, about 35–45 minutes from Sapporo for ¥800. The airport bus is the alternative: ¥1,500 to central Sapporo, about 65–70 minutes. In winter, when the roads are slow, the train is the safer bet.

The rule that catches visitors: every limited express seat is reserved

On JR Hokkaido, all seats on all limited express trains are reserved — there are no non-reserved seats. You need a reserved-seat ticket in addition to your basic fare ticket, and you must get it before boarding: you cannot buy a seat on the train. Get it at a JR ticket counter, a reserved-seat ticket machine, or online through JR-EAST Train Reservation.

The same rule applies if you hold a rail pass: the pass covers the fare, but you still need a reserved-seat ticket for each ride, and pass holders can reserve for free. Book early around the Snow Festival and New Year, when the popular trains sell out. There is also no onboard sales service on limited expresses, so buy food and drinks at the station first — and there is no oversized-luggage reservation system like the one on the Tokaido shinkansen, so luggage space is first come, first served. Travelling with skis or a big suitcase? Consider sending your bag ahead and travelling light.

For a first winter trip, the realistic day trips are Asahikawa, Noboribetsu and Otaru. Hakodate, Kushiro, Abashiri and Wakkanai are overnight trips — the distances are long, and in winter you do not want to be rushing for the last train home in the dark. Asahikawa is the gateway to Biei and Furano (a local train from Asahikawa, ¥680 to Biei and ¥1,380 to Furano).

The Hokkaido Rail Pass: when it pays

The Hokkaido Rail Pass covers unlimited rides on all JR Hokkaido lines — limited express, rapid and local trains, plus some JR Hokkaido buses — except the Hokkaido Shinkansen. It is sold to holders of a non-Japanese passport, including foreign residents of Japan, and it runs for 5, 7 or 10 consecutive days from a start date you choose. Reserved seats in ordinary cars are included.

Prices are adult fares at the time of writing (November 2026); children aged 6–11 pay half. Buying in advance — through JR-EAST Train Reservation or an overseas travel agent — saves ¥1,000 per adult. Collect or buy the pass at a designated counter, or at the passport-reader ticket machines at Sapporo Station and New Chitose Airport Station. It does not cover the Sapporo subway, the streetcar or Green Car seats, so keep an IC card for getting around the city.

Does it pay? JR Hokkaido's own example: a return trip Sapporo–Hakodate plus a return trip Sapporo–Otaru costs ¥23,140 in single tickets — already more than the 5-day pass at ¥22,000, before you ride anything else. A Sapporo–Asahikawa return alone is ¥10,880, so one long return plus day trips is roughly break-even; two long trips make it a clear saving. If you are only staying in Sapporo with one day trip to Otaru, skip the pass and pay as you go. The Hokkaido Rail Pass on Klook is rated 4.9 from over 5,600 reviews there, with 200,000+ bookings: Hokkaido Rail Pass on Klook (affiliate link). If you are coming from Tokyo rather than flying in, compare it with the nationwide pass in our rail pass guide first.

Staying closer to Sapporo? The Sapporo–Noboribetsu Area Pass (4 days, ¥10,000 in advance) and the Sapporo–Furano Area Pass (4 days, ¥11,000 in advance) cover the airport, Sapporo, Otaru and either Noboribetsu or Asahikawa–Furano.

The Sapporo Snow Festival by train

The 77th Sapporo Snow Festival runs from Thursday 4 February to Thursday 11 February 2027 (the last day is a national holiday), across three sites, all free to enter: Odori Park for the giant snow sculptures, Susukino for the ice sculptures, and Tsudome for snow slides and snow play. The five large snow sculptures stand in Odori blocks 4, 5, 7, 8 and 10, with the international snow sculpture contest at block 11. Detailed 2027 information is published on the official site from mid-December 2026, so check it before you go.

Getting there is easy once you are in Sapporo. Odori Park runs east–west through the city centre, and Odori Station on the subway sits right underneath it, between blocks 3 and 4. Susukino is one stop south on the Namboku Line. Tsudome is the exception: it is out in Higashi-ku, reached by the Toho Line to Sakaemachi Station and a shuttle bus from there — check the official site for the 2027 shuttle details when they are published.

Book early. Festival week is the busiest week of the year in Sapporo: hotels sell out months ahead, and the limited expresses into the city fill up, so reserve your seats as soon as your dates are fixed. If you have a spare evening, Otaru's Snow Light Path runs 6–13 February 2027, about 40 minutes away by train — candles in the snow along the canal, and a much quieter crowd than Odori.

Winter travel: a few things to know before you go

Build in buffer time. Hokkaido's trains are built for snow, and they keep running in weather that would stop a bus or a car — but heavy snow can still delay them, especially on the long single-track sections east and north. Do not plan a tight connection to a flight home on your last day.

Dress for the platform, not the train. The trains are warm, but you will wait on open platforms and walk between stations in sub-zero air. Warm boots with grip matter more than anything else: Sapporo's streets are icy.

Start from the airport train, pick one or two long rides, and let the pass do the rest. In winter the view from a warm limited express — snowfields, frozen coast, a plume of steam at Noboribetsu — is a large part of the trip.
