Year-End and New Year Trains in Japan 2026–27

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: if you are travelling by shinkansen between 25 December 2026 and 5 January 2027, book a reserved seat before you travel. Every Nozomi on the Tokaido and Sanyo Shinkansen runs all-reserved in that period, so a non-reserved ticket gets you no seat — at most a place to stand on the deck. Reserved seats go on sale at 10:00, one month before your travel date. In Tokyo, JR East keeps six lines running after the last train on New Year's Eve, so you can still get home after midnight.

New Year is Japan's biggest travel week of the year. The 2026–27 break runs from Saturday 26 December to Sunday 3 January — nine days — and millions of people ride home, then back again. JR adds extra trains and runs overnight trains in Tokyo for it. Here is what to book, and when.

The Nozomi rule: every seat reserved, 25 December to 5 January

JR Central and JR West run every Nozomi between Tokyo and Hakata with reserved seats only from Friday 25 December 2026 to Tuesday 5 January 2027. Normally cars 1 and 2 are non-reserved; in this period those 165 seats per train switch to reserved as well.

With a non-reserved ticket you can still board a Nozomi, but you cannot sit. JR's own guidance is to stand on the deck of an ordinary car — not the Green Cars — and if the train is too crowded, you may not be able to board at all. Follow the station staff if they hold you back; they are managing the platform, not being difficult.

Hikari, Kodama, Mizuho, Sakura and Tsubame keep their non-reserved cars as usual, though JR warns those fill up too. If the Nozomi you want is full, a Hikari is the fallback: it stops more often, but it still gets you to Kyoto or Osaka, and its non-reserved cars are a real option. When your seat goes on sale

Reserved seats go on sale at 10:00 Japan time, one month before your travel date — the same date in the previous month — at station counters and ticket machines, and through SmartEX, Eki-net, e5489 and the other JR online services. Set a reminder for that morning: the most popular departures on the peak days sell out fast.

Two quirks worth knowing. 31 December and 1 January tickets both go on sale on 1 December, because November has no 31st — JR's rule is that a date with no match in the previous month goes on sale on the 1st. And SmartEX and Express Reservation open earlier for some seats: eligible bookings can be made from 05:30, one year before travel, with the train and seat number confirmed from 08:00 one month before. Only some seats are available that early, and some products are excluded, so the one-month sale at 10:00 is still the date that matters for most people.

If the train you want is full

First, try a different time or a different day. JR adds trains for the period — JR East alone runs 6,882 extra trains from 1 December 2026 to 28 February 2027 (3,645 shinkansen, 2,486 limited express and 751 rapid trains), and JR Central says it adds trains linking the Tokaido and Sanyo Shinkansen directly. An early-morning or late-evening departure is often the one with seats left.

If you already hold a non-reserved ticket, change it to a reserved seat before boarding. You can change a ticket to a reserved seat once without a fee — you only pay the fare difference — at a major station counter or at some ticket machines. Do it before you go through the gate.

If you miss your reserved Nozomi, your ticket is not wasted. It still lets you stand on the deck of a later Nozomi, or take a non-reserved seat on a Hikari or Kodama on the same day. To sit on a Nozomi again, you need a new reserved-seat ticket.

Shifting your trip by a day or two helps twice. Seats are easier to get, and reserved-seat fares are cheaper outside the busiest season — JR Central charges different reserved-seat fares by season, and the year-end days are the most expensive. JR East also runs its trains on a holiday timetable from 30 December to 3 January, so check the times the day before rather than assuming weekday frequency.

New Year's Eve in Tokyo: trains after midnight

JR East runs overnight trains on six Tokyo-area lines after the last train on 31 December, into 1 January. This is for shrine visits and countdowns — and for getting home afterwards.

JR East also runs special trains in the early hours of 1 January for the first sunrise and first shrine visits — for example, a limited express leaves Shinjuku at 3:20 and reaches Awa-Kamogawa at 6:17, and the Fuji Excursion leaves Shinjuku at 4:13 for Kawaguchiko at 6:17. These are all reserved seats, and they are part of the same winter extra-train announcement, so book them the same way.

What it costs, and the JR Pass catch

Two things catch visitors at New Year. The first is the fare: reserved-seat express fares change with the season, and the year-end days are the busiest season, so the same seat costs more than on an ordinary day. The second is the pass. A Japan Rail Pass does not cover Nozomi or Mizuho trains — and at New Year that matters, because the Nozomi is the train most people want. Pass holders can still ride them by buying a Nozomi/Mizuho ticket for each ride, on sale from one month before travel to the day itself:

The price is the same for Green Car, reserved and non-reserved versions. During the all-reserved period, a non-reserved Nozomi/Mizuho ticket still only gets you a place to stand on the deck, so buy the reserved version. If you would rather not pay the extra, book a reserved seat on a Hikari or Kodama instead — the pass covers those in full, and JR recommends pass holders reserve ahead in this period. Pick your date, set a reminder for 10:00 one month before, and book the moment sales open — with a Hikari as your backup plan. That is the whole trick to New Year trains in Japan.
