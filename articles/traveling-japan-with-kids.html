Traveling Japan with Kids: Child Fares, Strollers and Family-Friendly Trains

Family travel guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: on JR and Tokyo Metro, children aged 6 to 11 pay a child fare (about half), toddlers aged 1 to 5 ride free with a paying passenger (up to two per adult or child), as do babies under 1. Strollers don't have to be folded on JR East trains, and on the Tokaido, Sanyo, Kyushu and Nishi Kyushu shinkansen the oversized-baggage rule doesn't apply to strollers (though using the oversized-baggage area still needs a reservation). If you want a guaranteed seat for a small child, book a reserved seat, and remember that a child under 6 who takes a reserved seat of their own needs a child ticket.

Below are the rules that matter most for families, taken from the railway companies' own pages, plus a few practical tips.

Who pays what: the age bands

JR and Tokyo Metro use the same age groups. The one twist is that school year matters as well as age.

GroupAgeFare
Adult12 and overAdult fare. A 12-year-old who is still in elementary school counts as a child.
Child6 to 11Child fare, half the adult fare (see the exceptions below). A 6-year-old who hasn't started elementary school yet counts as a toddler.
Toddler1 to 5Free when accompanied, within the limits below
InfantUnder 1Free

JR East says child tickets, limited express tickets and reserved seat tickets are half the adult price, but adds that in some cases they are not half. JR West spells out one exception: Green Car tickets, GranClass tickets and sleeper tickets cost the same for a child as for an adult. So a child's trip in the Green Car costs more than half the adult total.

Tokyo Metro also explains its rounding: the child fare is half the adult fare, "rounded up to the nearest 10-yen unit or rounded down to the nearest 1-yen unit if an IC card is used."

When toddlers and babies need a ticket

Free does not mean unlimited. On JR, a child fare is needed for a toddler or infant in these cases:

  • A third toddler. One adult or child can bring up to two toddlers free. From the third one, you pay a child fare.
  • A reserved seat of their own. If a toddler or infant uses a reserved seat, a Green Car seat (except in a non-reserved Green Car) or a sleeper berth on their own, a child fare is needed.
  • Traveling alone. A toddler traveling alone pays a child fare.

JR East's English FAQ puts the seat rule simply: "Children under 6 years old will need a ticket or pass to secure a reserved seat." So the child-fare rule is tied to a toddler taking a seat of their own; one sharing your seat rides free (within the two-toddler limit). On a long shinkansen ride with a 4- or 5-year-old, though, a seat of their own can be worth the child fare.

Tokyo Metro works the same way: "Up to two toddlers can ride the subway free of charge as long as they are accompanied by an adult or a child." A third toddler pays a child fare, and babies under 1 ride free.

JR Pass and IC cards for children

Japan Rail Pass. The official site defines children as "those aged 6 to 11 at the time of use", and adds that "elementary school students aged 12 or older are considered “adults”". That's stricter than the normal JR fare rule above. One helpful detail: for online purchases, a child who is 11 on the payment date is treated as a child even if they turn 12 before using the pass. At the time of writing (October 2026), child prices when bought online are ¥25,000 (7 days), ¥40,000 (14 days) and ¥50,000 (21 days) for the ordinary pass, half the adult price. Passes bought through overseas agents cost a little more (¥27,000 for a 7-day child pass). Our JR Pass calculator and rail pass guide help you check whether a pass is worth it at all.

One catch for families: JR Pass seat reservations don't cover everything. The official conditions say "Shinkansen TRAIN DESK cars, Cars for passengers traveling with children, and S Work cars are not covered."

Welcome Suica (Child). JR East's card for visitors has a child version, which "can be used until the March 31st after the child's 12th birthday." A family member can buy it for the child. JR East says, "Your child needs to have an ID (passport, etc.) ready for verification purposes", and at a vending machine you enter the child's date of birth. It's valid for 28 days including the day you buy it. You can get it at JR East Travel Service Centers (including Narita Airport Terminal 1, Narita Terminal 2·3, Haneda Terminal 3, Tokyo, Shinjuku, Shibuya, Ikebukuro and Ueno) and at Welcome Suica vending machines at the Narita Airport Terminal 1, Narita Terminal 2·3 and Haneda Terminal 3 stations. More in our Welcome Suica guide.

TOURIST PASMO. PASMO's visitor card also has a child version, which "can only be used up to March 31 of the year that the child turns 12", and is valid for 28 days from purchase. For how all the IC cards compare, see Suica, PASMO and ICOCA explained.

Tokyo day passes. Child prices on Toei's ticket page include ¥500 for the 24-hour Tokyo Subway Ticket and ¥350 for the Toei 1Day Pass, at the time of writing.

Strollers on trains and the shinkansen

A 2014 council of Japan's transport ministry set the basic policy that strollers can be used on public transport without folding them, and JR East's luggage guide lists strollers as "Folding not required." JR East adds: "Some trains have an open area for wheelchairs and strollers." If there isn't one, "use the space at your feet," and "Be careful to avoid inconveniencing other passengers, especially when the train is crowded." Tokyo Metro similarly asks stroller users "not to obstruct the path of other commuters", and asks other passengers to be understanding.

The same ministry guidance asks parents to:

  • not keep the child in the stroller when using escalators;
  • always set the wheel brakes, keep a hand on the stroller and keep an eye on it;
  • use the shoulder and waist seat belts.

On the Tokaido, Sanyo, Kyushu and Nishi Kyushu shinkansen, bags with total dimensions between 160 cm and 250 cm need an oversized-baggage reservation. JR Central says, though, that "the oversized baggage rules do not apply to strollers". However, "a reservation is required if you wish to use an oversized baggage area or an oversized baggage compartment." JR East's own family tips suggest booking a seat in the last row of the car and putting the stroller in the space behind it. On the Tokaido Shinkansen, JR Central says the seats in the last row of some cars are "seats with oversized baggage area", so book one of those if you want to use that space (other lines may differ, so check). If you're also traveling with big suitcases, sending them ahead makes stations much easier. Our shinkansen guide covers the rest.

Seats, nursing and diaper changing

Book a reserved seat. JR East's advice for traveling with a baby is to reserve a seat so you're sure to sit down. If you book on Klook instead of a JR site, the Tokyo to Kyoto shinkansen e-ticket (affiliate link) is listed from US$85.30 on Klook at the time of writing; check the ticket options for children on the listing before you book.

Nursing on the shinkansen. JR East says you can ask the conductor or crew to use the multi-purpose room on board, but it isn't always available, so bring a nursing cover just in case. Diapers can be changed in the toilets with baby changing tables. On JR Central's trains, the multi-purpose room is described as "primarily for disabled passengers". The easiest plan is the one JR East suggests: feed and change before boarding, in a station baby room.

Baby rooms and stroller rental at stations. JR East lists the stations and station buildings where it has nursing spaces, diaper-changing beds and parent-and-child toilets, and also lists Babycal, a paid stroller rental service at stations. Tokyo Metro has a list of stations with facilities for infants or small children inside the gates. Toei's Children's Smile Spot at Ueno-okachimachi Station (Oedo Line), outside the ticket gates, opened in 2023; Toei's announcement gave the nursing room's hours as 8:00 to 20:00, and it also has Babycal stroller rental and vending machines for diapers and baby supplies.

Priority seats and women-only cars. Priority seats on Tokyo Metro and JR East are for people including those with infants or children, and pregnant passengers. During the morning rush (from the first train to around 9:30), Tokyo Metro's women-only cars can also be used by "elementary school students and younger children". If you can, avoid the rush entirely. Our rush hour guide has the times.

A quick family checklist

  • Count your toddlers: two ride free per paying passenger, and a third needs a child fare.
  • Want a reserved seat for a child under 6? Buy them a child ticket.
  • Bring your child's passport if you're buying a child Welcome Suica.
  • For a JR Pass, a 12-year-old counts as an adult, even if they're still in elementary school (unless you bought online while they were 11).
  • Don't keep your child in the stroller on escalators; set the brakes and keep a hand on the stroller.
  • Feed and change before boarding a long train, and think about where the stroller will go when you book your seats.
