Nara Day Trip from Kyoto or Osaka: Which Train, and What to See

Day-trip guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: Nara is an easy half-day or day trip. From Kyoto, take the Kintetsu limited express (about 35 minutes, extra fee) or the JR Miyakoji Rapid (about 45 minutes, covered by JR passes). From Osaka, Kintetsu from Osaka-Namba takes about 40 minutes, and JR from Osaka Station about 50. Kintetsu-Nara Station is the closer one to Nara Park and Todai-ji.

The travel times below come from Nara City's official tourism site, and the temple hours and fees from Todai-ji's own website.

JR or Kintetsu? Pick by where you start

Two different railway companies run to Nara, and they arrive at two different stations: JR Nara and Kintetsu-Nara. Neither is wrong, but they suit different travelers.

Since March 2025 there is also the JR limited express Mahoroba, linking Osaka and Nara via Shin-Osaka. There's no direct bus from Kyoto or Osaka, so the train is the way to go.

Which one should you take?

  • You have a JR pass: take JR. JR passes don't cover Kintetsu trains. If you're spending several days around Kyoto, Osaka and Nara, the JR Kansai Area Pass on Klook (affiliate link) covers the JR routes in the table above.
  • You're staying near Namba in Osaka: Kintetsu from Osaka-Namba is the simplest.
  • You want the shortest walk to the deer and temples: Kintetsu-Nara Station is nearer to Nara Park. From JR Nara, you can also take the city loop bus.

Getting around once you arrive

According to Nara City's tourism site, a loop bus runs frequently every day from both JR Nara and Kintetsu-Nara stations to the main sights around Nara Park and the city center. Many visitors simply walk, since the park starts close to Kintetsu-Nara Station. You can pay for trains with an IC card such as Suica or ICOCA. See our IC card guide.

Todai-ji and the Great Buddha: hours and fees

Todai-ji's Great Buddha Hall (Daibutsu-den) is the highlight for most visitors. These are the temple's own figures at the time of writing (October 2026):

April–OctoberNovember–March
Great Buddha Hall7:30–17:308:00–17:00
Todai-ji Museum9:30–17:30 (last entry 17:00)9:30–17:00 (last entry 16:30)
  • Admission: ¥800 for adults (and ages 13–18), ¥400 for ages 6–12.
  • Combined ticket (Great Buddha Hall + Museum): ¥1,200 for adults, ¥600 for ages 6–12. It's sold at both the hall and the museum.
  • Audio guide: ¥500, rented just past the Great Buddha Hall ticket gate. No booking needed for individuals.

In autumn and winter the hall opens later and closes earlier, so plan to arrive before mid-afternoon.

Nara Park and the deer

Nara City's tourism site says Nara Park is home to around 1,300 deer. They wander freely through the park and around the temples. They're wild animals, so keep food and paper maps out of reach, and follow the signs in the park.

Would a day tour be easier?

If you want to see Nara together with other famous spots in one day without planning trains, a guided tour can help. The Kyoto & Nara day tour on Klook (affiliate link) combines Nara Park with Fushimi Inari, Arashiyama and the Sagano train. It was rated 4.9 from about 10,900 reviews on Klook at the time of writing. Prices vary by date and option, so check the current price before booking. If you'd rather go at your own pace, the train is simple and cheap.

A simple half-day plan

  1. Take the train to Kintetsu-Nara (or JR Nara and the loop bus).
  2. Walk through Nara Park to Todai-ji and the Great Buddha Hall.
  3. Spend some time with the deer on the way back, then head back to Kyoto or Osaka.
