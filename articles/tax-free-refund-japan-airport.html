Tax-Free Shopping in Japan from November 2026: How the New Airport Refund Works

Shopping & airport guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: For purchases made on or after 1 November 2026, you pay the full price with tax at the shop, and the tax is refunded later. Before that, Customs at your departure airport has to confirm that you're taking the goods out of Japan. That means one extra stop: a passport scan at a tax refund kiosk in the departure lobby, before you check in your bags. Keep your purchases with you until then, don't use them up in Japan, and get to the airport with time to spare.

The Japan Tourism Agency calls this the "Refund Method". It replaces the old system, where the shop simply didn't charge you the tax. Here's what it means for you at the shop and at the airport.

What changed on 1 November 2026

Under the new rules, the tax is refunded "after a customs inspection at the airport or seaport confirms that the goods are being taken out of Japan." The old system covers purchases up to 31 October 2026. The National Tax Agency says the date you bought the goods decides which system applies, not the date you leave, and that there's no transition period where both systems run side by side.

Who can use it, and what counts

  • Who: the Japan Tourism Agency lists foreign nationals who are not residents and "can confirm that they have been in Japan for less than six months since entry." Some Japanese nationals living abroad also qualify. Diplomats, official personnel and US military are excluded.
  • Minimum spend: "Purchases totaling 5,000 yen or more (tax excluded) per store per day are eligible."
  • What: goods you take out of Japan yourself when you leave. Gold and platinum bullion, and goods that are already exempt from consumption tax, are excluded.
  • Consumables: the old split between general goods and consumables is gone, and so is the special sealed bag. But the agency warns: "if consumables such as food, beverages, or cosmetics are consumed in Japan, you will not be eligible." If you've opened and used something, don't scan it at the kiosk. Tell a customs officer at the counter instead.

At the shop

Shop at a store that offers tax-free sales and show your passport at the till, as before. The difference is that you pay the full price, including consumption tax. The tax is refunded later by the shop or by "a refund service provider entrusted by the shop."

How you get the money back depends on the shop. The Japan Tourism Agency lists "bank transfers, credit card transfers, app-based refunds, or cash refunds" as possible methods, with cash paid at your departure airport after Customs has confirmed your goods. Ask at the shop which method it uses. Official pages don't say how many days a refund takes, and say that questions go to the shop where you bought the goods.

At the airport: step by step

  1. Go to a tax refund kiosk in the international departure lobby, before you check in. The Japan Tourism Agency puts it bluntly: "you cannot retrieve luggage once it has been checked in with the airline." So pack your tax-free goods in your carry-on, or keep them out until the check is done.
  2. Scan your passport. The agency says the kiosk step "takes only a few seconds." You must do this "within 90 days from the date of purchase."
  3. Green result: "the customs inspection procedure is complete." You can check in.
  4. Red result: "you must bring your tax-free goods to the designated customs inspection area for verification." Inspections are done per receipt, and if one item from a receipt is missing, none of the goods on that receipt qualify.
  5. Collect your refund by the method the shop set up. At some airports there are also self-service refund machines (see below).

Do it at the airport you finally leave Japan from. If you take a domestic flight to connect to your international flight, do the procedure at the departure airport for your international flight, not the domestic one.

Allow extra time. The Japan Tourism Agency asks you to "arrive at the airport or seaport with sufficient time" and warns that "neither the airline nor Customs will provide compensation" if you miss your flight. Kansai Customs adds that "the tax refund kiosk at the airport might be congested." No official source gives a set number of minutes, so build in a buffer when you plan your train to the airport. Skip the queue with Visit Japan Web

At Narita, Haneda, Kansai, Chubu, Fukuoka, New Chitose and Naha, you can do the customs confirmation on your phone with Visit Japan Web instead of the kiosk. It only works in the international departure lobby, inside the dedicated Wi-Fi area, before security. Japan Customs says "you must set up and be ready to use VJW before undergoing customs confirmation."

The National Tax Agency's guide lists the steps: register your details on Visit Japan Web (account, passport and landing permit), connect to the dedicated Wi-Fi (Customs gives the network name as "Customs_WiFi_For_TaxRefund"), tap "Refund Procedure", check your purchase records and tap "Confirm". If a record shows "Not Completed", go to the customs inspection area. Tap "Confirm" before you check your bags. If you can't finish online, Customs says to use a kiosk.

What we know about Narita, Haneda and Kansai

  • Kansai: Osaka Customs' guide shows Terminal 1, 4th floor, with 12 tax refund kiosks, a customs inspection area and a Wi-Fi area for Visit Japan Web. Terminal 2 has 4 kiosks, an inspection area and a Wi-Fi area near the check-in lobby.
  • Haneda and Narita self-service refund machines: Haneda's terminal operator announced a "Self Tax Refund" service from 1 November 2026 at Haneda, Narita and Naha. Where your shop's refund method allows it, you can take the refund in cash (Japanese yen) from a machine in "approximately one minute." The operator lists the machines at:
    • Haneda Terminal 2: 3F departure area, after immigration, 6:45–25:30 (may change with flight schedules)
    • Haneda Terminal 3: 3F departure area, after immigration, 24 hours
    • Narita Terminal 1: 4F Central Building, before departure formalities, 6:00–24:00
    • Narita Terminal 2: 3F Main Building, before departure formalities, 6:00–24:00

The same notice says the locations of the kiosks and refund machines "vary by airport" and asks you to check the airport's website before you go. If you're flying from Haneda, our Haneda terminals guide will help you work out which terminal you need.

Practical tips from the rules

  • Keep each shop's receipt with its goods. Customs checks per receipt, and a missing item can cancel the refund for the whole receipt.
  • Don't eat, drink or use up the snacks, drinks or cosmetics you bought tax-free if you want the refund. Consumables used in Japan don't qualify; if you've used part of something, tell a customs officer rather than scanning it.
  • Pack tax-free goods in your carry-on, or keep them out until the kiosk is done. You can't get checked bags back.
  • Sending bags ahead? If you use luggage forwarding to the airport, make sure your tax-free purchases are with you when you go to the kiosk, because the check happens before check-in.
  • Mind the 90 days. You need to present your passport at a terminal within 90 days of the purchase date.
