Understanding Haneda's Runway Direction: How to Predict the Best Spotting Conditions

Spotting technique · How the airport works

If you've ever visited Haneda twice and found that one day the planes came in perfectly over one deck, and the next day nothing lined up at all, you weren't imagining it. Haneda switches which runways it uses depending on the day's wind direction — and that changes which observation deck actually gives you the best view. Understanding this one rule can save you a wasted trip.

Why wind direction changes the runway in use

Aircraft take off and land into the wind for stability, so airports switch their active runways to match the day's wind. Haneda has four runways, labeled A, B, C, and D, and operates in one of two broad configurations: "north wind operations" or "south wind operations."

North wind operations

When the wind is from the north, Runway A (on the west side) and Runway C (on the east side) are the primary runways in use. Landing aircraft tend to approach from the southeast — roughly from the direction of the Tokyo Bay Aqua-Line — which means you'll see inbound planes coming in from over the bay.

South wind operations

When the wind is from the south, Runway B (north side) and Runway D (south side) become the primary runways. In this configuration, landing aircraft tend to approach from the northeast, roughly from the direction of Tokyo Disneyland — a completely different angle than north wind operations.

What to check before you go

Since the approach direction depends on which configuration is active that day, it's worth checking the wind direction before heading out — even a quick glance at a weather app's wind indicator helps. Once you've been a few times, you'll start to be able to look at the wind direction and guess which terminal's deck is going to have the better angle that day, without needing to check anything else.
