Kyushu by Rail from Fukuoka: Yufuin and Beppu

Sightseeing guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: base yourself at Hakata Station in Fukuoka and both of Oita's famous onsen towns are easy day trips by train. Take the green sightseeing train Yufuin no Mori to Yufuin one day, and the limited express Sonic to Beppu the next. The Yufuin no Mori sells out, so book its seats the moment sales open, one month ahead at 10:00. If you do both trips, the Northern Kyushu Rail Pass (3 days, ¥15,000) costs less than the two return tickets bought separately.

Yufuin and Beppu sit about 30 km apart in Oita Prefecture, but they feel like different worlds. Yufuin is a small, quiet town of galleries, cafes and ryokan under Mount Yufu. Beppu is a bigger, steamier resort city, famous for its seven "hells" — boiling springs you look at rather than bathe in. The trains are part of the trip in both directions, which is why this works so well from Fukuoka.

Day 1: the Yufuin no Mori to Yufuin

The Yufuin no Mori is JR Kyushu's sightseeing train to Yufuin: a dark-green train with a wood-panelled interior, big panoramic windows and a lounge car open to all passengers. It runs through the mountains on the Kyudai Main Line, past the Mino Mountains and the Jion-no-taki Falls. There are three departures a day from Hakata:

Coming back, trains leave Yufuin at 12:01, 15:56 and 17:17, arriving at Hakata at 14:19, 18:10 and 19:27. The 15:56 departure starts in Beppu at 14:36. For a day trip, take No. 1 out and No. 6 back: that gives you about five and a half hours in Yufuin.

Every seat is reserved — there is no standing or unreserved option. A one-way adult ticket from Hakata to Yufuin costs ¥6,130 at the regular fare, or ¥5,600 with JR Kyushu's online-only Kyushu Net Kippu. Seats go on sale one month before the travel date at 10:00 Japan time on the JR Kyushu Train Reservation site, and JR Kyushu says seats are "consistently fully booked day after day". Set a reminder for 10:00 on the opening day, especially for weekends and autumn. On some days only Nos. 1, 2, 5 and 6 run, with the Yufu covering the No. 3 and 4 slots — check the operating calendar on JR Kyushu's page before you book.

If it is sold out: the limited express Yufu runs the same route three times a day with ordinary cars, and one car is non-reserved, so you can usually walk up and ride. JR Kyushu also suggests going via Kokura and Oita instead: the Sonic to Oita, then a local train up the Kyudai Line to Yufuin.

In Yufuin: Yunotsubo street runs from the station to Lake Kinrin, lined with small shops and cafes, and the lake — with its little shrine gate in the water and Mount Yufu behind it — is the classic photo stop. Yufuin Station has a free footbath on platform 1, a good place to wait for the train home. The train's bento boxes are by advance reservation only.

Getting from Yufuin to Beppu

Staying overnight and seeing both towns in one trip is easy. By local train, ride from Yufuin down the Kyudai Line to Oita, change to the Nippo Main Line, and continue to Beppu — about 1 hour 15 minutes in total, according to JNTO. A Kamenoi local bus (route 36) takes about 50 minutes to an hour. The Yufuin no Mori No. 3 also runs direct from Yufuin to Beppu, arriving 13:31, but only on days it operates. Send your big suitcase ahead if you are moving hotels (see our luggage forwarding guide) and travel light between the towns.

Day 2: the Sonic to Beppu

The Sonic is the workhorse of this route: a limited express from Hakata to Beppu and Oita via Kokura, about 31 trains a day, roughly every 30 minutes in the daytime. The fastest trips take 2 hours 1 minute. An adult one-way ticket costs ¥6,910 with a reserved seat or ¥6,380 without one, at the time of writing (November 2026). JR Kyushu's online-only tickets are much cheaper: the Kyushu Net Kippu is ¥3,500, and the Kyushu Net Hayatoku 3 is ¥2,950 if you book at least three days ahead. Unlike the Yufuin no Mori, the Sonic rarely needs booking weeks ahead — but reserve a seat on busy weekends.

The last Sonic back leaves Beppu at 21:38 and reaches Hakata at 23:51, so a full day in Beppu is easy. A morning departure around 9:00 puts you in Beppu before 11:00, leaving five or six hours for the town.

In Beppu: start with the seven Hells (Jigoku Meguri) in the Kannawa district — the cobalt-blue Umi Jigoku and the red Chinoike Jigoku are the most photographed. One combination ticket covers all seven hells: ¥2,400 for adults and ¥1,200 for elementary and junior high school students, valid on the day of purchase and the next day. All seven are open 8:00 to 17:00 every day of the year. Kamenoi Bus runs from Beppu Station to Kannawa in about 20 minutes. After the hells, save time for a bath: the station area is full of public baths and ryokan. If you only have one afternoon, do the hells first and bathe last.

Does the Northern Kyushu Rail Pass pay off?

Yes, if you do both day trips within three days. The JR Kyushu Rail Pass (Northern Kyushu Area) costs ¥15,000 for 3 days or ¥17,000 for 5 days for an adult — ¥1,000 less on JR Kyushu's own online booking. It covers the Yufuin no Mori, the Yufu, the Sonic and other JR Kyushu trains in Fukuoka, Saga, Nagasaki, Oita and Kumamoto, with up to six seat reservations.

Two things to know. First, the pass covers the trains, but the Yufuin no Mori seat still has to be reserved — do it as soon as you have the pass, since the seats sell out either way. Second, for one trip only the pass does not pay: a single Yufuin return (¥12,260) or Beppu return (¥13,820) is cheaper than ¥15,000, and the online-only Kyushu Net Kippu tickets are cheaper still. The pass wins when you do both trips, or add a third day out — Nagasaki and Kumamoto are in the same area. Our regional JR passes guide compares it with the other Kyushu and nationwide passes.

Quick checklist

  • Book the Yufuin no Mori exactly one month ahead at 10:00 JST; No. 1 out (9:17) and No. 6 back (17:17) make the best day trip.
  • If it is full, take the Yufu (non-reserved car available) or go via Kokura and Oita on the Sonic.
  • Check the Yufuin no Mori operating calendar — Nos. 3 and 4 do not run every day.
  • For Beppu, the Sonic runs about every 30 minutes; the last train back leaves Beppu at 21:38.
  • Buy the ¥2,400 Hells combination ticket at the first hell you visit; it is valid for two days.
  • Doing both trips in three days? The Northern Kyushu Rail Pass (¥15,000) beats ¥26,080 in single tickets. Doing one trip only? Buy the online-only Kyushu Net Kippu instead.
  • Carry cash for the hells and small shops in Yufuin, and keep an IC card topped up for buses and local trains.
