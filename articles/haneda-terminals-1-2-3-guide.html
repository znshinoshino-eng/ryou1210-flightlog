Haneda Airport Terminals 1, 2 and 3: Which One You Need and How to Move Between Them

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: Terminal 1 is domestic (JAL and a few others), Terminal 2 is ANA's terminal and handles both domestic and some ANA international flights, and Terminal 3 is the main international terminal. A free shuttle bus links all three, and if you are connecting between flights you can ride the train between terminals for free with a transit ticket from the information counter.

Haneda is the closest airport to central Tokyo, but "Haneda" on your ticket isn't enough: the three terminals are far enough apart that going to the wrong one can cost you real time. Trains and airport buses also stop at each terminal separately, so knowing your terminal tells you where to get off. Here's how to work it out and how to get between them.

Which terminal does your flight use?

According to the airport's official FAQ, the split looks like this:

TerminalFlightsAirlines
Terminal 1Domestic onlyJapan Airlines (JAL), Skymark Airlines, StarFlyer, Japan Transocean Air
Terminal 2Domestic and internationalDomestic: ANA, AIRDO, Solaseed Air. International: ANA
Terminal 3International onlyMany airlines, including JAL's international flights

Two things catch people out:

  • ANA international flights can use Terminal 2 or Terminal 3. ANA says both terminals are used for ANA-operated international flights, and the terminal can vary by destination. Other airlines' flights, including code-shares sold under an ANA number, use Terminal 3.
  • Code-shares follow the operating airline. The airport notes that "other rules may apply" for code-share flights. For example, StarFlyer flights sold as ANA code-shares use Terminal 1, not Terminal 2.

How to check: ANA's Flight Status page shows the departure terminal from two days before departure; if your ANA booking shows "(0)" or "-" for the terminal, it could still be either Terminal 2 or 3. For arrivals, ANA confirms the terminal about two hours before landing. For other airlines, check your airline's booking details or the flight search on the official Haneda Airport website.

Getting to the right terminal by train or bus

Both rail lines into Haneda stop at every terminal, so the main job is getting off at the right station.

  • Tokyo Monorail (from Hamamatsucho) has three airport stations: Haneda Airport Terminal 3, Haneda Airport Terminal 1 and Haneda Airport Terminal 2, in that order heading into the airport. From Hamamatsucho, the Monorail lists 13 minutes to Terminal 3 on the Haneda Express (17 on a local), 16 to Terminal 1 (22 on a local) and 18 to Terminal 2 (24 on a local).
  • Keikyu Line (from Shinagawa and Yokohama) has two airport stations: Haneda Airport Terminal 3, and one shared station for Terminals 1 and 2. At that shared station, follow the signs to Terminal 1 or Terminal 2.
  • Airport limousine buses usually stop at Terminal 2, then Terminal 1, then Terminal 3 on the way into Haneda, though some routes run in a different order and the operator warns that some buses don't stop at every terminal. Check the timetable for your route.

Moving between terminals: the free shuttle bus

The simplest way between terminals is the free inter-terminal shuttle bus. It leaves from the arrival lobby floor of each terminal:

  • Terminal 1: 1st floor (Arrival Lobby), Bus Stop 8
  • Terminal 2: 1st floor (Arrival Lobby), Bus Stop 9
  • Terminal 3: 1st floor, Bus Stop 0 (for buses to Terminals 1 and 2)

The timetable in effect from 18 August 2026 (the airport says "for the time being") is:

RouteFirst busLast bus
Terminal 1 → Terminal 24:551:23
Terminal 1 → Terminal 35:041:32
Terminal 2 → Terminal 1 → Terminal 35:001:28
Terminal 3 → Terminal 1 → Terminal 24:471:15

Buses run every 4 minutes between 5:00 and 20:00, and every 8 minutes between 20:00 and midnight. Road congestion can delay them, so leave yourself some slack. Crew can help wheelchair users on board, and no booking is needed.

What about walking from Terminal 1 to Terminal 2? There is an underground passage with moving walkways of about 400 m between the two, but at the time of writing the airport says it is out of service because of construction work. Check the airport's "Travel between terminals" page for the current situation, and use the shuttle bus in the meantime.

Moving between terminals by train

The Monorail and Keikyu both connect the terminals, which is handy if you are already standing on the platform or the bus queue is long.

  • Tokyo Monorail fares between airport stations (at the time of writing, October 2026): Terminal 3 to Terminal 1 or 2 is 230 yen with a paper ticket (229 yen by IC card); Terminal 1 to Terminal 2 is 180 yen (177 yen by IC card). Child fares are 120 yen (114 yen by IC card) and 90 yen (88 yen by IC card) respectively.
  • Free for connecting passengers: if you are connecting between a domestic and an international flight, or between two international flights, you can ride Keikyu or the Monorail between Terminal 1 or 2 and Terminal 3 for free with a "Transit Boarding Ticket". Pick it up at the Information Counter (Terminal 1 B1F, Terminal 2 B1F or Terminal 3 2F); staff will check your passport and boarding pass.

For everyday trips, an IC card such as Suica or PASMO is the easiest way to pay. See our IC card guide.

Connecting between domestic and international flights

This is where knowing the terminals matters most. When you arrive on an international flight and connect to a domestic one, you go through immigration and customs at Haneda first, and JAL notes that it has no baggage transport service between the terminals.

AirlineRouteTime the airline suggests
JALDomestic (T1) → international (T3)About 70 minutes
JALInternational (T3) → domestic (T1)At least 1 hour 20 minutes
ANAInternational → international (T2/T3)75 minutes
ANAInternational → domestic80 minutes

These are the airlines' own guide times rather than official minimum connecting times, so give yourself more if you can, especially at busy times.

  • JAL, international to domestic: JAL has a domestic transfer counter on Level 2 (arrivals) of Terminal 3, open 5:30–11:00 and 13:30–19:45, where you can check in bags for a same-day domestic flight until 60 minutes before departure. Be through security at Terminal 1 at least 20 minutes before departure.
  • ANA, international to domestic: ANA's domestic transfer counter in Terminal 3 is open 5:30–19:30 and closes 60 minutes before departure; outside those hours, use Counter 14 in Terminal 2. The domestic transit security check in Terminal 3 is open 5:30–11:00 and 13:30–19:45; when it is closed, take the free shuttle bus to Terminal 2.
  • ANA, domestic to international within Terminal 2: follow the international connection signs up to the departure lobby on the 3rd floor.

The international departures area in Terminal 2 opens at 5:00 AM, which matters if you are on an early ANA flight from there.

A few tips from a railway worker

  • Check your terminal before you board the train, not when you arrive. Getting off at the right station saves you the shuttle ride.
