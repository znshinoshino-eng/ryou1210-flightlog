Tap Your Credit Card to Ride: Which Trains in Japan Accept Contactless Cards

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: in Tokyo, Osaka, Kyoto and Fukuoka, many subways and private railways now let you tap a contactless credit or debit card at the ticket gate, or the same card in Apple Pay or Google Pay. That includes Keikyu to Haneda Airport and Nankai to Kansai Airport. JR East does not accept it, so that rules out the Yamanote Line and the Narita Express. Coverage changes fast. Everything below was checked on the operators' own websites in October 2026.

It's a handy backup, and for a short trip it can replace an IC card on some routes. It comes with more rules than Suica, though, and they're easy to trip over. Here's what works, where, and what to watch out for.

What tap-to-ride is, and how it differs from Suica

You tap a contactless card (credit, debit or prepaid) on a dedicated reader at the gate when you enter, and again when you exit. Your phone or watch works too if the card is set up on it. There's no registration and no topping up. The fare is charged to your card, and each day's rides are added together and billed as one charge the next day, according to Toei and Kintetsu. You can see your trip history on the Q-move website if you register there.

How it differs from an IC card like Suica:

  • It only works on operators that have signed up. Suica works on almost every train and bus in the big cities. Tap-to-ride doesn't.
  • Adult fare only. There's no child fare. A child can use a card in their own name, but pays the adult fare.
  • One card, one person. Only the cardholder may use it, so you can't pay for your travel partner with the same card.
  • It's the regular fare. Operators describe it as the standard adult fare in 10-yen units. In the Tokyo area, the only discount is ¥70 when you change between Tokyo Metro and Toei subway lines.

For how IC cards work, see our guide to Suica, PASMO and ICOCA.

Find the reader, and which cards work

The reader is a separate pad on the gate with the contactless symbol (the four curved lines). It isn't the round IC card target. Not every gate has one. Some operators have only certain gates, or a separate terminal standing next to the gates. Hold your card or phone on it until you hear the beep (Tokyo Metro). Kintetsu asks for at least one second.

Your card needs the contactless symbol. Kintetsu notes that some cards with the symbol still won't work, so check with your card issuer if you're unsure. The 11 Tokyo-area operators accept Visa, Mastercard, JCB, American Express, Diners Club, Discover and UnionPay on a physical card. On a phone, Tokyo Metro and Tokyu list only Visa, Mastercard, JCB, American Express and Discover. Tokyu names Apple Pay, Google Pay, Garmin Pay and Fitbit Pay, and warns that some phones may not work.

Does JR East accept it? No

At the time of writing (October 2026), JR East isn't part of the scheme. Toei's English guide says JR East, Keisei and Hokuso don't accept tap-to-ride. Tokyo Metro tells you to use an IC card on JR East. In practice, that means the Yamanote Line, Chuo Line, Narita Express and JR East shinkansen all need Suica, a ticket or a rail pass.

The same goes for Narita Airport. Toei says tap-to-ride isn't available at Narita Airport or Airport Terminal 2 stations. At Haneda, Keikyu works. See our Haneda and Narita guides for the best ticket into the city.

Things to watch out for

These rules come from the operators' own pages:

  • Only participating lines, stations and gates. If you ride onto a line that doesn't take it, such as JR East, Tokyo Metro says the tap is cancelled for the whole trip. You then pay in cash, in two separate settlements. Some connecting gates don't take it either. At Kintetsu's JR West connection at Tsuruhashi and its Meitetsu connection at Kintetsu-Nagoya, you must exit and re-enter.
  • Same card, same device, in and out. Tapping in with your card and out with your phone isn't allowed, even if it's the same card number. Nankai and Fukuoka also count the card and the phone separately for their daily caps.
  • Transfers cost more. The 11 Tokyo-area operators work out one fare across lines, but Tokyu says normal transfer discounts don't apply. If you change between Tokyo Metro and Toei outside the gates and take more than 60 minutes, you pay a new fare.
  • Passes and discount tickets don't mix. You can't combine tap-to-ride with commuter passes, day passes or special tickets. If you have a Tokyo Subway Ticket, use the ticket instead.
  • Shinkansen and limited express trains. JR Kyushu says it can't be used for the shinkansen. None of the official pages I checked say tap-to-ride covers limited express or reserved-seat charges, so ask station staff before boarding a Romancecar or a rapi:t. For the shinkansen, see our shinkansen guide.
  • It can't be used to pay a fare difference at a fare adjustment machine (Toei, Kintetsu), and it's valid only on the day you enter.
  • Tap firmly. If a transfer gate doesn't read your card, the wrong fare may be charged at your destination. If no entry or exit is recorded, Kintetsu may ask you to pay the fare in cash.
  • Mistakes aren't refunded. If you tap the wrong card, Tokyo Metro says the record can't be cancelled.
  • Disruptions. Tap-to-ride isn't covered by replacement transport when a line stops running. Kintetsu warns it may also be unavailable during gate or system failures.

And my own advice from the gates:

  • Present one card. Take the card you want to pay with out of your wallet, rather than tapping a wallet full of cards, so the gate reads the right one.
  • Watch your phone battery. Because you must exit with the same device, a phone that dies mid-ride is a real problem. A power bank helps (see our eSIM, Wi-Fi and power bank guide).
  • Check your bank's foreign transaction fees. Every ride is a card charge in yen.
  • Keep an IC card as a backup. You'll need it for JR East, many buses and anything not in the table above. Our Kyoto and Osaka guide covers the Kansai side.
