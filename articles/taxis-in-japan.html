Taxis in Japan: Apps, Tokyo Fares and Airport Flat Fares

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked October 2026

Short answer: taxis in Japan are clean, safe and easy to use, even if you don't speak Japanese. Hail one on the street, find a taxi stand at a station, or use an app like GO, which works in English and accepts foreign phone numbers. In Tokyo the meter starts at ¥500, there's a 20% surcharge from 22:00 to 5:00, and tolls are extra. From the airports, Narita has fixed fares by zone and Haneda has flat-rate taxis to most of Tokyo.

I work on the railways, so I'll usually tell you to take the train. But a taxi does make sense sometimes: late at night, with a lot of luggage, in a group of three or four, or for the last short hop to a hotel that's far from the station. Here's how they work, based on what the official sources say.

How to catch a taxi

  • On the street: in big cities, taxis are everywhere. Japan's national tourism organization (JNTO) explains that the sign on the front of the car is colour-coded: red means the taxi is free and green means it's occupied.
  • At stations: in smaller towns, taxis usually wait in front of the train station.
  • By app: useful when there are no taxis passing, at busy times, or when you'd rather not explain your destination out loud (more on apps below).

Three things that surprise first-time visitors:

  • The doors open and close automatically. JNTO says not to handle them yourself, so stand back and let the driver open the rear door.
  • No tipping. According to JNTO, taxi drivers don't expect or accept tips.
  • Have your destination in Japanese. Hotel names and big sights are easy, but for anything else, show the address or a map on your phone.

Credit cards can generally be used in big-city taxis, but JNTO notes that in the countryside cash may be the only option. Carry some yen just in case.

Taxi fares in Tokyo (from April 2026)

Fares are set by region, so they're different in Osaka, Kyoto and elsewhere. Tokyo's fares were revised on 20 April 2026. For a standard taxi in Tokyo's 23 wards and the cities of Musashino and Mitaka, the meter works like this at the time of writing (October 2026):

ItemTokyo 23 wards, Musashino, Mitaka
Starting fare¥500 for the first 1.0 km
After that¥100 for every 232 m
Slow trafficA time charge is added when the taxi is moving very slowly
Late night / early morning20% extra from 22:00 to 5:00
Expressway tollsPaid by the passenger, on top of the fare

If you call or book a taxi to come to you, there can be an extra pick-up or booking charge on top of the meter. Each taxi company sets its own.

For most trips inside central Tokyo, the train is still far cheaper. Our guide to how Tokyo's train network works explains the lines, and an IC card like Suica or PASMO makes the trains easy.

Taxi apps: GO and others

JNTO lists four taxi apps that offer English-language service: GO, S.RIDE, Uber and DiDi. Coverage differs from app to app and city to city, so it's worth installing one or two before your trip and checking your destinations.

Here's what GO's official App Store listing says:

  • The app is available in English, Japanese, Korean, and Simplified and Traditional Chinese.
  • You can sign up with a non-Japanese mobile number.
  • It covers all 47 prefectures. You set your destination in the app, so you don't need to explain it to the driver.
  • With GO Pay, you register a credit card (Visa, Mastercard, JCB, American Express or Diners Club) and pay in the app, but it isn't available in every taxi.
  • In certain urban areas you can reserve a taxi from 15 minutes to 7 days ahead.
  • Fees for reservations and the app itself may be added in some areas, so check the fare breakdown in the app before you confirm.

Apps need a data connection. If you haven't sorted that out yet, see our eSIM, pocket WiFi and power bank guide.

Narita Airport: fixed fares by zone

Narita Airport lists fixed taxi fares to Tokyo's 23 wards, Mitaka and Musashino. The price depends on the zone and the expressway route. These are the fares before tolls, as shown on the airport's taxi page at the time of writing (October 2026):

ZoneMain areasVia Keiyo RoadVia Bayshore route
AEdogawa, Katsushika, Adachi¥23,500¥26,500
BKoto, Sumida, Daiba¥28,000¥28,000
CChuo, Chiyoda, Taito, Bunkyo, Arakawa¥29,000¥31,000
DShinjuku, Shibuya, Minato, Shinagawa, Toshima, Nakano, Meguro, Ota¥31,500¥32,500
EItabashi, Kita, Nerima¥34,000¥36,000
FSetagaya, Suginami, Mitaka, Musashino¥35,500¥38,500
  • Expressway tolls are paid on top. A late-night and early-morning surcharge applies from 22:00 to 5:00.
  • The fixed fare assumes an expressway route. If you ask for ordinary roads, the meter is used instead.
  • No reservation is needed. Use the fixed-fare taxi stand at your terminal and confirm the fare with the driver before you get in.

Narita is a long way from central Tokyo, so the train or airport bus costs a fraction of this. Our Narita to Tokyo guide compares them.

Haneda Airport: flat-rate taxis

Haneda is much closer to the city, so a taxi is a more realistic option there. The airport says flat-rate taxis run between Haneda and most of Tokyo and Kanagawa, with some areas still using the meter. The Tokyo Hire-Taxi Association explains how the Tokyo side works:

  • From Haneda: no reservation is needed at the terminal taxi stands. The flat rate doesn't cover the Tama area from the stands; that's metered unless you book ahead with a company that serves Tama.
  • To Haneda: book with a taxi company at least one hour before pick-up. If you just hail a taxi in the street, you pay the normal meter.
  • Tolls are added on top, and a 20% premium applies if the whole trip is between 22:00 and 5:00.
  • Generally one pick-up point and one drop-off point. A big detour or a change of destination switches you back to the meter.

If you've booked through an app, go to the separate app and reservation stands rather than the regular taxi stands on the 1st floor. The airport lists them as: Terminal 1, 2nd floor (departures) near Exit 7; Terminal 2, 2nd floor near Exit 2; and Terminal 3, the 8th floor of P5 parking. The airport warns that pick-up points can change when it's busy, so check the app. For trains and buses from Haneda, see our Haneda to Tokyo guide.

Taxi, train or pre-booked transfer?

  • Train: cheapest and often fastest in the city, but hard work with big suitcases at rush hour.
  • Taxi: door to door, good for short hops, groups and late nights. Watch the late-night surcharge and the tolls.
  • Pre-booked transfer: a driver meets you at arrivals at an agreed price, which is handy with a family, lots of luggage or a late landing. If luggage is the real problem, you can also send your suitcases ahead and take the train. See our luggage forwarding guide. And if you're landing late, our guide to arriving late at Narita or Haneda covers last trains as well.

Quick checklist

  • Install a taxi app before you fly and make sure your phone has data.
  • Save your hotel's name and address in Japanese.
  • Look for the red sign when hailing, and let the door open by itself.
  • Carry some cash, especially outside big cities.
  • From Narita, confirm the fixed fare and your zone before you get in. From Haneda, use the right stand.
  • Remember: no tipping, and tolls and the night surcharge are extra.
