Kansai Passes Compared: Osaka Amazing Pass, Kansai Railway Pass or Pay as You Go

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: the Kansai Thru Pass no longer exists. Its sales ended with fiscal year 2023, and its successor is the Kansai Railway Pass. The pass on sale online for 2026–27 is the digital-only Kansai Railway Pass LITE. For a sightseeing-heavy day inside Osaka, the Osaka Amazing Pass (¥3,500 for one day) can pay off if you really use the free attractions. For a simple day trip to Kyoto or Nara, an IC card and single fares, or a cheap one-company pass, usually beat both. Prices below are at the time of writing (November 2026) and can change.

First, what happened to the Kansai Thru Pass

A lot of older guides still recommend the Kansai Thru Pass. Its operator, Surutto Kansai, announced in February 2024 that the pass's sales would end with the fiscal year 2023 sales period, and that a new Kansai Railway Pass for overseas visitors would go on sale from 1 April 2024.

Two other things changed recently, so check the date on anything you read:

  • The JR Kansai Area Pass is no longer sold. JR West's page says "Sales ended as of September 30, 2026," and that passes bought by that date "will remain valid throughout the specified validity period."
  • The Osaka Amazing Pass 2-day covers private railways too. The official site lists Hankyu, Hanshin, Keihan, Kintetsu and Nankai trains "mainly running in Osaka City for 1day and 2day", so check that any guide you read matches.

Osaka Amazing Pass: when it pays off

The pass gives unlimited rides on Osaka Metro and Osaka City Bus (the site notes "Some routes are excluded"), the New Tram, and the private railways mainly inside Osaka City, plus free entry to "approximately 40 sightseeing spots". It costs ¥3,500 for one day or ¥5,000 for two consecutive days, and the official site says "Prices are the same for adults and children". Children's passes aren't sold, so a child needs an adult pass.

The honest test is simple. Osaka Metro's own day ticket, the Enjoy Eco Card, costs ¥820 on weekdays and ¥620 on weekends and holidays, covering the subway and city buses (with a few exceptions). So the Amazing Pass only wins if the attractions you'll actually visit that day add up to more than the difference, ¥2,680–¥2,880. Check each attraction's own admission price before you buy, and be realistic about how many you can fit in.

Things that catch people out:

  • It's an e-ticket on your phone. The site says the tickets "will not be available in physical form", and screenshots or printouts of the QR code are not accepted. Buy it online (for example through the Surutto QRtto app); over the counter, it's sold only at the Kansai Tourist Information Center.
  • A "day" runs from 3:00 a.m. to 2:59 a.m. Tap "Start Using" after 3:00 a.m. on your travel day. Start it just after midnight and it stops working at 3:00 a.m. The 2-day version is for two consecutive days.
  • One visit per attraction. "You can enter each facility once on the day you use the train and bus". On the 2-day pass, it's once per attraction across both days.
  • Some attractions need a time slot. LEGOLAND Discovery Center needs an advance reservation on its own website, and the Tsutenkaku observation deck needs a time slot booked at the facility. The Umeda Sky Building's Kuchu Teien Observatory is free with the pass only until 15:00. After that you get 10% off.
  • Certain reserved-seat limited expresses cost extra, and it doesn't cover airport limousine buses. There's a separate Kansai Airport version (¥5,300, valid for 28 hours) that adds one Nankai ride from the airport to Namba.
  • Refunds: unused passes can be refunded through the website you bought from. Used passes can't, even for bad weather or closures.

The current passes are on sale until 31 March 2027. If you'd rather book through a travel site, it's also sold as the Osaka Amazing Pass on Klook (affiliate link), from US$20.75 on Klook at the time of writing (prices vary by date and option).

Deal until 31 December 2026: code OAP5OFFYEAREND takes 5% off the Osaka Amazing Pass on Klook (up to US$3 off, limited number, first come first served; not for residents of Korea).

Kansai Railway Pass LITE: for long private-railway days

The LITE pass is a digital-only pass from the same operator as the old Thru Pass. Its page says "you can board metro and private railways across Kansai as much as you want". It costs ¥5,200 for two consecutive days or ¥6,500 for three consecutive days, and it's on sale from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. It's "Online sales only", through travel sites (the operator's page links to Klook, KKday, WAUG and Trip.com), and it's an e-ticket.

Read the exclusions before you buy. In the operator's words: "The pass does not cover JR, KYOTO CITY SUBWAY, KEIHAN ELECTRIC RAILWAY (Keishin Line and Ishiyama-sakamoto Line), RANDEN (KEIFUKU RAILROAD) and buses." Kyoto City's tourism site words the Keihan exclusion more broadly, so if your plan relies on Keihan, check the operator's page first. Reserved-seat express trains on Kintetsu, Nankai, Keihan and Hankyu cost extra, and there's "no refund" once you start using it.

That means no JR trains, and in Kyoto no city buses or subway, which many visitors rely on in Kyoto. The pass makes sense when you chain long private-railway rides on consecutive days. Osaka to Kyoto by Hankyu one day, Nara by Kintetsu the next, Kobe after that. Our Kyoto and Osaka getting-around guide covers the local buses and subways you'd still pay for separately.

Pay as you go: often the cheapest for one day trip

With an IC card such as ICOCA, Suica or PASMO, you just tap in and out. Kyoto City Bus accepts "ICOCA, Suica, PASMO, & PiTaPa", and Hankyu accepts IC cards too. See our IC card guide for how to get one. Some official single fares at the time of writing:

Osaka Metro Enjoy Eco Card (1 day)¥820 weekdays / ¥620 weekends & holidays

Here's the comparison for a classic Kyoto day from central Osaka. Keihan from Yodoyabashi to Gion-Shijo and back is ¥980, and three city bus rides within the flat-fare zone add ¥690. That's about ¥1,670 for the day, well under the ¥2,600-a-day cost of a 2-day LITE pass, which wouldn't cover those buses anyway. Hankyu says its limited express trains need only the ordinary fare, with no separate limited express charge. Reserved seats are a different matter: the pass pages list Hankyu reserved-seat trains as extra.

If you'd like one ticket, a single-company pass can fit a single day trip:

  • Keihan Kyoto-Osaka Sightseeing Pass: ¥1,650 for one day, or ¥1,850 for 24 hours. Kyoto City's tourism site says it covers all Keihan lines except the Otsu Line and is only for foreign visitors on a "short-term stay". It's worth it if you hop off along the way, for example at Fushimi-Inari, rather than riding straight there and back.
  • Kintetsu Rail Pass: ¥1,900 for one day or ¥3,700 for two consecutive days, covering Kintetsu in the Osaka, Nara and Kyoto areas. The 2-day version adds Nara Kotsu buses within designated zones. Kintetsu notes that the pass "alone does not allow you to board Limited Express trains". See our Nara day-trip guide for the route.

Which one should you pick?

  • A full sightseeing day in Osaka, several paid attractions: Osaka Amazing Pass, if their admissions add up to more than the ¥2,680–¥2,880 gap.
  • A light Osaka day, mostly walking and food: Enjoy Eco Card or just an IC card.
  • One day trip to Kyoto or Nara: IC card, or the Keihan or Kintetsu pass if you'll stop along the way.
  • Two or three long private-railway days in a row (Kyoto, Nara, Kobe): Kansai Railway Pass LITE, as long as you don't need JR or buses.
  • Lots of JR trips, such as Kansai Airport by Haruka or further afield: look at JR's regional passes in our regional JR pass guide, since the JR Kansai Area Pass has ended.
