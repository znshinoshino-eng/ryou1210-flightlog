Hakone Freepass vs Fuji-Hakone Pass: Which One Pays Off?

Transit guide · Written by someone who works on Japan's railways · Checked November 2026

Short answer: if your trip is Hakone only, buy the Hakone Freepass. The Fuji-Hakone Pass costs ¥3,600 more than the 3-day Freepass and only pays off if you actually go on to the Fuji Five Lakes and ride the Fujikyu Railway both ways, plus the buses around the lakes. Both passes cover the same Hakone loop and the same Odakyu round trip from Shinjuku — the Fuji pass just adds the Fuji side. And it does not cover the JR train back from Otsuki to Shinjuku, which is the gap that catches people out.

Prices are at the time of writing (November 2026) and can change. Both passes count calendar days, not 48 hours: the day you buy is day one, and a 2-day pass ends after the last train or bus on the following day. The Odawara Freepass covers the Hakone area only — it has no Odakyu round trip — so buy that version if you are arriving by shinkansen from Kyoto or Osaka and don't need a Shinjuku leg at all.

What the Hakone Freepass covers

From Shinjuku, the Freepass includes one round trip on the Odakyu Line between your departure station and Odawara — one trip out, one trip back, not unlimited rides. Inside Hakone it gives unlimited rides on eight kinds of transport: the Hakone Tozan Train, the Hakone Tozan Cable Car, the Hakone Ropeway, the Hakone Sightseeing Cruise (the pirate ship on Lake Ashi), the Hakone Tozan Bus and the KANKO SHISETSU-MEGURI sightseeing bus, plus the Odakyu Highway Bus and Tokai Bus on designated routes. It also gets you discounts at more than 70 attractions around Hakone.

The one thing it does not include is the Romancecar, Odakyu's reserved-seat limited express. That costs an extra ¥1,200 per adult (¥600 per child) each way between Shinjuku and Hakone-Yumoto, or ¥1,150 if you book online through EMot. It takes about 80 minutes, against about two hours by ordinary trains with a change at Odawara, and every seat is reserved, so book from 10:00 one month ahead if you want the panoramic front seats. Changes and refunds are only possible before departure, with a ¥100 handling fee on refunds.

You can buy the Freepass at Odakyu station ticket machines and windows, or online through EMot as a digital ticket on your phone. You can also buy it in advance through Hakone Freepass on Klook (affiliate link), rated 4.6 from more than 11,700 reviews, from about US$44.95 at the time of writing.

What the Fuji-Hakone Pass adds — and what it doesn't

The Fuji-Hakone Pass covers everything the Freepass covers, then adds the whole Fujikyu Railway between Otsuki and Kawaguchiko and Fujikyu buses in the designated areas around Mt Fuji. It includes the same discounted Odakyu round trip between Shinjuku and Odawara, runs for three consecutive days, and a part of the proceeds is donated to Mt Fuji conservation.

What it does not cover is just as important: the JR Chuo Line between Shinjuku and Otsuki, the highway buses from Shinjuku to Kawaguchiko, and the Romancecar seat fee. That first gap shapes your return journey. From Kawaguchiko you ride the Fujikyu Railway to Otsuki (covered), then you pay the JR train from Otsuki back to Shinjuku yourself — a local train takes about 100 minutes. The faster option is the direct Fuji Excursion limited express from Kawaguchiko to Shinjuku, about ¥4,200 one way with reserved seats only.

Buying it is also different. The Fuji-Hakone Pass is a paper ticket only, sold at the Odakyu Sightseeing Service Centers at Shinjuku West or Odawara — not from ticket machines or online. Bring your passport: Odakyu sells it to visitors to Japan and asks to see a non-Japanese passport or residence card. Klook sells a voucher you exchange for the paper ticket at the service center — the Fuji-Hakone Pass on Klook (affiliate link) is rated 4.7 from 379 reviews, with more than 10,000 booked, at US$70.25 at the time of writing.

The maths: when the extra ¥3,600 pays off

The honest comparison is not ¥11,100 against ¥7,500. Both passes include the same Odakyu round trip and the same Hakone loop, so the only question is whether the Fuji side is worth the ¥3,600 difference. Here is what that ¥3,600 buys, at single-fare prices:

What the extra ¥3,600 buysPaying separately
Fujikyu Railway, Otsuki → Kawaguchiko, one way¥1,170
Fujikyu Railway, Kawaguchiko → Otsuki, one way¥1,170
Fujikyu buses around the lakes, unlimited for the tripPaid per ride

The train return alone is ¥2,340, so you need roughly ¥1,260 more value from the lake buses to break even — easy if you spend a full day hopping between Kawaguchiko, the ropeway and the Chureito Pagoda area, but not if you just ride the train in and out. The pass loses if you only ride the Fujikyu line one way, for example by taking the highway bus back to Shinjuku, or if you only get as far as Gotemba and never reach the lakes. And remember the Fuji pass has no 2-day version: for a Hakone-only trip of two days, the 2-day Freepass at ¥7,100 is the cheapest ticket that covers the loop.

A 3-day circuit that makes the Fuji-Hakone Pass worth it

Day 1 — Hakone. Shinjuku to Hakone-Yumoto, then the classic loop: the mountain train up to Gora, the cable car, the ropeway over the steaming Owakudani valley, and the pirate ship across Lake Ashi. Stay overnight in Hakone — our onsen towns guide covers the area.

Day 2 — over to the lakes. From Hakone to Gotemba and on to Kawaguchiko by bus, then the Fujikyu buses around the lake and the Fujikyu line to Shimoyoshida for the Chureito Pagoda. Stay at the lake so you get the early-morning view of Fuji, when it is clearest.

Day 3 — back to Tokyo. Fujikyu Railway from Kawaguchiko to Otsuki (covered), then the JR Chuo Line to Shinjuku (not covered — pay separately), or the direct Fuji Excursion if you booked a seat. One practical note: not every bus around the lakes is inside the pass's designated areas, so check which routes are covered at the service center when you buy.

Which one should you pick?

  • Hakone only, one or two days: the 2-day Hakone Freepass from Shinjuku (¥7,100).
  • Hakone only, three days: the 3-day Freepass (¥7,500) — the third day costs just ¥400 more.
  • Hakone plus the Fuji Five Lakes in three days, riding the Fujikyu line both ways and using the lake buses: the Fuji-Hakone Pass (¥11,100).
  • Arriving by shinkansen from Kyoto or Osaka: the Odawara Freepass (¥6,000 for two days) — don't pay for a Shinjuku round trip you won't use.
  • Short on time, or Fuji only: skip both passes and take the Fuji Excursion train or a highway bus direct to Kawaguchiko instead.
