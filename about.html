About Flightlog Japan

Who this is for

Flightlog Japan is written for visitors to Japan who are into aviation — plane spotters, avgeeks, or anyone who just wants a good photo of a jumbo jet against a Tokyo sunset. The goal is simple: tell you exactly where to go, how to get there, and when, without the vague "visit a famous spot" advice you get from generic travel sites.

Who's behind it

This site is run by a Tokyo-based photographer who shoots at Haneda regularly (Sony α7 IV with a SIGMA telephoto lens) and also has hands-on, detailed knowledge of how Japan's train and railway system works — so the access and ticketing advice here comes from real, practical experience, not guesswork. More photography content (in Japanese) is published on note.com under the name "ryou1210."

Why we link to Klook

Most of the tickets and passes in our guides link to Klook. Here's why we chose it, and where its limits are.

  • You can book in your own language and currency. Many Japanese operators only sell online in Japanese, or only at a station counter. Klook lets you book in English (and other languages) and pay in your own currency before you fly.
  • Most bookings are confirmed instantly. You usually get a QR code or voucher on your phone straight away, so you know your plan is set before you land.
  • Prices are usually the same as the official price, or close to it. Some products are slightly discounted, some aren't. We check prices when we write each guide and show them with the date we checked.
  • There are lots of real reviews. The popular products have thousands of reviews, which helps you spot problems (a confusing pickup point, a slow exchange counter) before you book.
  • Some products offer free cancellation. Not all of them, so check the terms on each listing before you pay.

Where Klook isn't the best choice: for everyday train rides, an IC card (Suica, PASMO or ICOCA) is simpler and cheaper than any booking. When buying at the station or on the official site is just as easy or cheaper, our guides say so.
